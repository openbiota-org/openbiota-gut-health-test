## 1. Binding product objective

Build `openbiota fmt match`. It must reuse available results JSON, analytical caches and FASTQ-derived measurements; derive additional features when necessary; compare every donor against the recipient; and evaluate combinations of donors for complementary restoration potential. Return transparent individual rankings, alternative donor sets, their incremental contributions, and specific transmission concerns. Explain exclusions using source-qualified evidence. Allow zero, some or all candidates to be excluded; never force a winner.

The intended use is **experimental computational matching**. It is useful even when no clinical matching model exists for the recipient's condition. Implement the transparent comparison engine first and enhance it with published predictive and mechanistic models. Do not make a proprietary model, an author email, an external immune assay, a complete clinical history, or a human randomized matching trial a prerequisite for calculating observable complementarity.

The output must distinguish these questions:

1. What potentially useful organisms, strains and functions does each donor supply that the recipient does not demonstrably have at the desired level?
2. Which supplied features are plausibly compatible with the recipient's existing community, and what can a validated model predict about their persistence?
3. What does an additional donor contribute beyond the best individual donor, and what uncertainty or unwanted exposure does that addition introduce?
4. What specific infectious, resistance, toxin or mechanistic disease concerns are supported by the available evidence?
5. Which conclusions concern measured composition, predicted engraftment, simulated metabolism or actual clinical outcomes?

A donor's absence of disease-like scores does not establish safety. A favorable matching index does not establish clinical benefit. These distinctions must appear next to the relevant result, while the tool still performs the requested research comparisons.

**Do not generate physical preparation, stool-mixing ratios, administration instructions, antimicrobial conditioning regimens or an authorization to perform FMT.** Donor sets in this release are computational hypotheses. Their composition, viability and behavior after physical combination cannot be inferred by concatenating donor FASTQs. Actual administration and material release belong to the responsible clinical/research program.

### 1.3 What this release must actually compute

Required local core: identity/provenance checks; compatible taxon and function comparisons; recipient target ledger; individual donor coverage; distance and overlap descriptors; qualified concern ledger; exhaustive or bounded donor-set search; marginal gains; scenario sensitivity; deterministic ranking explanations; JSON plus HTML/PDF report.

Required research extensions: strain-resolution comparison where reads support it; a reproducible public-data engraftment model; metabolic complementarity through a pinned community model; co-engraftment/interaction features; adapters for newer published matching methods when their required artifacts are available. Each extension has a fallback and an explicit capability status. An extension is not “implemented” merely because its paper is linked.

## 2. What the evidence supports

### 2.1 Endpoint map and the matching evidence hierarchy

Use independent endpoint enums, never one generic `success` label:

- `feature_available_in_donor`: a supported observation in the sequenced donor material.
- `post_fmt_species_presence`: a species is detected after exposure; origin may be unresolved.
- `donor_strain_engraftment`: donor-specific evidence appears in a recipient at a stated time.
- `persistent_donor_strain`: donor attribution persists across prespecified follow-up times.
- `community_convergence`: recipient composition becomes more similar to donor composition under a defined metric.
- `functional_capacity_change`: a genetic pathway/capacity changes.
- `modeled_exchange_flux`: a metabolic model output under stated medium and constraints.
- `measured_metabolite_change`: an actual assay result.
- `clinical_response`: a defined indication-specific patient outcome.
- `donor_derived_adverse_event`: a transmission/adverse-outcome claim with its attribution evidence.

An algorithm can predict one endpoint well and be poor at another. Every model output must carry endpoint, time horizon, population, assay, validation design and calibration status. A probability is only a probability for its trained and calibrated endpoint. An index scaled to 0–100 is an index.

### 2.3 Evidence for multiple donors: preserve the actual exposure

| ID | Study / observation | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| P01 | FOCUS UC RCT: pooled multidonor FMT, primary outcome 11/41 versus 3/40 placebo. [Paramsothy 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28214091/). | Evidence for that intervention versus placebo; not a randomized comparison of one versus several donors. |
| P02 | UC trial: pooled donor FMT versus autologous FMT, eight-week steroid-free remission 12/38 versus 3/35. [Costello 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30644982/). | Supports the studied pooled intervention; does not isolate an optimal donor count. |

| P04 | MicroPouch non-pooled multidonor RCT changed donor similarity but not remission relative to placebo. [Primary trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38708959/). | Require outcome-specific evaluation; do not label community movement itself a cure. |
| P05 | MINDFUL, a small early-terminated UC trial, found donor-dependent effects; fiber changed engraftment without improving the clinical outcome. [Gogokhia 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40460824/). | Diet and recipient context can modify ecology. No automatic pre-FMT fiber or conditioning instructions. |
| P06 | Kazerouni/Wein modeled donor selection under latent effective-donor assumptions; its treatment strategies included cycling donors. [Primary mathematical model](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0163956). | A mathematical rationale for exploring alternatives, not empirical pooled-engraftment validation. |

Store `exposure_type = single | physical_pool | nonpooled_multidonor | sequential | defined_consortium | unknown`. A donor-set coverage calculation is permutation-invariant; a sequential exposure is not. Never relabel a sequential dataset as pooled training data. Do not estimate a physical-pooling benefit by comparing heterogeneous studies as though participants had been randomized by donor count.

## 4. CLI and integration contract

### 4.1 Commands

Implement these commands in the existing CLI framework; equivalent flag spelling is acceptable only if help and tests document it. Examples show interfaces to build, not currently existing commands.

```bash
openbiota fmt match \
  --recipient /data/SAMPLE2_A02 \
  --donor SAMPLE6=/data/SAMPLE6_A06 \
  --donor SAMPLE4=/data/SAMPLE4_A04 \
  --indication long_covid \
  --out /data/fmt/SAMPLE2_v7

openbiota fmt match --manifest /data/match-request.json --out /data/fmt/run1

openbiota fmt inspect --manifest /data/match-request.json

openbiota fmt explain --run /data/fmt/run1 --candidate SAMPLE4+SAMPLE6

openbiota fmt validate --run /data/fmt/run1

openbiota fmt models list

openbiota fmt models train --recipe public-strain-baseline-v1 --cohorts /data/fmt-cohorts

openbiota fmt evaluate --model /models/strain-v1 --split leave-study-out

openbiota fmt followup --baseline-run /data/fmt/run1 --recipient-followup /data/followup
```

Additional flags: `--cache-root`, `--reuse-only`, `--allow-reprocess`, `--models core|standard|all-available`, `--goals FILE`, `--screening-manifest FILE`, `--max-subsets`, `--max-donors-per-set`, `--seed`, `--threads`, `--memory-gb`, `--offline`, `--formats json,html,pdf`, `--redact-labels`. Add `--objective`, `--endpoint`, `--horizon`, `--scenario-set` and `--max-materials-per-set` as defined in §§9–10. Default `--models standard` uses installed compatible artifacts and falls back to the core. An unavailable optional model does not fail a complete core report.

`--max-donors-per-set` is an optional research search constraint, not a biological safety limit. Without it, consider every size allowed by the search budget. Explain truncation. Do not silently default to only one donor or suppress all combinations.

Default outputs are `match.json`, `match.html`, `match.pdf` and an internal provenance/cache manifest under the chosen run directory. These are outputs of the implemented application; the coding-agent handoff remains this single specification. PDF/HTML must render from the same canonical JSON without independent score calculations.

Exit codes: `0` completed comparison, including no admissible candidate; `2` invalid request/identity conflict; `3` no usable analytical inputs; `4` mandatory explicitly requested model failed; `5` report integrity failure. Missing optional capabilities yield structured warnings and exit 0 if a meaningful core comparison was completed. A zero-donor request produces a recipient target/requirements report, no fabricated ranking.

### 4.3 Input discovery and trust order

Search only provided sample directories and explicitly configured cache roots. Record files considered, chosen and rejected. Order of preference: native typed measurement artifacts with provenance; validated native results JSON; analytical tables with complete namespace/denominator metadata; PDF observations. A report PDF is never a substitute for raw evidence when establishing an exact pathogen, toxin locus or donor strain.

Valid cached artifacts take precedence over reprocessing. Verify sample IDs, file digests, tool/database IDs, parameters, reference manifest and creation lineage. A cache with an unknown parent FASTQ is usable only with that provenance gap, not automatically equivalent to current input reads. If a profiler upgrade is needed, process all compared samples through the same pinned lane; do not mix one upgraded donor with older recipient abundances.

Inputs may include host-filtered FASTQs, taxonomic profiles, HUMAnN/DIAMOND panels, BAM/CRAM mappings, marker alignments, assemblies/MAGs, v05 determinant evidence, v06 intervention dossiers, metabolomics, qPCR/absolute biomass, sample metadata and clinical screening records. Do not require every modality. Give each an observed, unavailable or incompatible status.

PDF-only mode must work: extract cited page-level observations, flag ambiguous parsing, show conditional coverage/contrast tables and unresolved concerns, and identify the exact native artifacts needed to improve the comparison. It cannot produce calibrated strain probabilities, locus-confirmed hazards or an actual pooled composition. Never silently OCR a number into an uncited precise measurement.

## 6. Reuse and derive the analytical feature layers

### 6.1 Taxa and functional capacities

Use a canonical identity layer with source namespaces preserved. Prefer exact species/strain IDs; curate synonyms such as *Ruminococcus gnavus* → *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* and *Eubacterium rectale* → *Agathobacter rectalis* without equating profiler abundance scales. Resolve complexes explicitly, including *E. coli/Shigella*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae* complex and *F. prausnitzii* lineages. Log ambiguous many-to-one mappings.

Freeze the recipient goal lane and reference independently of the donor list, choosing the best validated recipient lane for each modality. Prefer donor artifacts compatible with that frozen lane; incompatible donor layers become unknown/incompatible, with separate descriptive comparisons. An explicitly requested harmonized all-sample rerun creates a new versioned target compilation; adding a donor alone cannot silently change the recipient lane or goals. Gene/pathway matching requires common reference IDs, normalized units, breadth/identity rules and denominator support. Reuse v05 determinant calls with their evidence; never infer toxin presence from a generic disease panel title.

For every functional pathway distinguish: a complete known route; partial genes; alternative routes; an isolated homolog; community-level complementary reactions; and a model-inferred route. A community can cross-feed metabolites, but fragments from unrelated organisms do not establish that any organism has a complete toxin operon or biosynthetic island. Terminal-gene panels such as reported but/buk capacity are valid separately named partial-capacity features and may contribute to an explicit terminal-gene goal. Do not require whole-route completeness merely to display or compare those measured genes; do not relabel them complete pathways.

### 6.2 Strain comparison

Implement an adapter around a pinned supported strain tool and preserve its limitations. Prefer one primary method for routine consistency and a second method for high-value validation, not a majority vote of incompatible definitions. Candidate tools and resources are in §14.

Before FMT, compute donor–recipient and donor–donor strain relatedness only for species with adequate callable breadth/depth under the tool's validated settings. Outputs: comparable genome fraction; callable sites; private/shared variants; strain distance; multiple-strain ambiguity; donor-specific marker support; and unresolved fraction. If species are below resolution, return `not_resolved`, not “different strains.”

Maintain three possibilities: a donor has a species absent/unresolved in the recipient; both carry distinguishable strains; or source attribution is ambiguous. Presence of the same species is not proof of identical strains or inevitable competitive exclusion. Related donors and common strains can make attribution impossible even with post-FMT data.

The follow-up command must compare recipient baseline, all actual source donors/materials, follow-up samples and unrelated-donor controls. Report donor-derived, baseline-persistent, new-source-unresolved and ambiguous strain categories. Without a pre-FMT sample, do not identify a newly detected strain as donor-derived merely because it is common in a donor.

### 6.3 Additional modalities

| Modality | Useful input | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Bacterial/archaeal taxonomy | Available niches, overlap, context and explicit target coverage | Universal organism goodness, absolute biomass or clinical benefit. |
| Fungi and eukaryotes | Qualified detectable organisms, ecological context and hazards | Viability, all parasites, or negative clearance. |
| Virome/phages | DNA-virus/phage features, host-linked hypotheses and source tracking where supported | RNA-virus absence from ordinary DNA sequencing; phage benefit from abundance alone. |
| Resistome/virulome | Exact determinants, context, mobile-element links and concerns | Phenotypic resistance or organism attribution from unlinked family counts. |
| Metabolomics | Measured concentrations and recipient/donor biochemical context | Equivalent in-vivo production or recipient response after transfer. |

| Host immune assay | Actual GMLR or other validated assay output | An inferred immune measurement reconstructed from microbial DNA. |
| Diet, medications, bowel transit, indication | Model applicability and ecological scenarios | Causal matching effects without evidence; default absence when unspecified. |
| Processing/storage/lot facts | Relationship between sequenced sample and available material | Live anaerobe survival from a historical DNA profile. |

### 6.4 Cache and computation graph

Cache key = hash of input artifact digests, sample/material lineage, tool/container/database IDs, parameters, identity mappings, reference bundle, goal policy, evidence-rule version and model artifact IDs. Separate expensive read-derived caches from cheap goal-dependent ranking caches. Changing goals reruns target/search/report stages; it must not unnecessarily rerun host filtering or taxonomic profiling.

Default to local data processing. Do not upload FASTQ, patient labels or clinical records to public model APIs. Online research/artifact retrieval is separate from sample inference. Validate checksums and licenses before importing model/code/data artifacts. Never execute downloaded notebooks merely to discover their inputs.

## 7. Recipient goals and the transparent matching engine

### 7.1 Build the target ledger before looking for a winner

Build the recipient target ledger once, independent of the donor list. Otherwise adding a donor can change what the recipient is said to need. Inputs are compatible recipient observations, actual reference eligibility, indication-specific evidence and explicit research goals. Every target must state why its direction was chosen.

Default rules:

1. A reference-supported low feature can create a `conditional` supply/range target. Record whether the reference is appropriate for this recipient and whether abundance is relative or absolute.
2. A common reference organism not detected in the recipient can create a candidate presence target. It is not a universal requirement for health. Retain detection uncertainty.
3. An explicit user goal can activate a feature without clinical trial evidence. Label the goal `explicit_user_goal` and preserve the evidence and uncertainty; do not present it as a validated treatment endpoint.
4. An indication hypothesis can create a mechanistic target when its direction is supported by a source. A disease classifier's arbitrary weight is not automatically a therapeutic weight.
5. Neutral or unknown-polarity organisms, age-adjusted disease indices, and general “high is bad” assumptions are context only by default.
6. A high recipient feature becomes `avoid_introduction` only when its adverse/excess direction is source-supported or explicitly selected as a research goal. Beneficial, neutral or unknown-polarity high features remain context/monitor observations. A donor lacking the feature is not evidence that it will eradicate it. No negative abundance credit is permitted.
7. Desired ranges are finite when biology warrants them. Do not maximize all SCFAs, secondary bile acids, diversity, mucin degradation, immune activity or total gene counts.
8. One underlying signal must not receive independent votes through a species, guild, pathway, disease score, GMWI2 and adjusted age. Build a `counting_group` graph; aggregate correlated components inside a capped goal group. Summary scores are explanatory views, not additional objective inputs.

If no valid reference exists, still compare explicit goals and observed donor supply, with the reference gap visible. The output is then goal-relative matching, not restoration to a proven healthy state. The number of analyzable donors must not depend on finding an age-matched reference for every feature.

### 7.2 Feature availability and target coverage

For donor material \(d\) and scenario-eligible scoring target \(j\), construct a **supported availability index** \(a_{dj}\in[0,1]\). This measures the donor's evidence for supplying that target under a defined rule. It is not engraftment probability or the fraction of a deficient organism that will be restored.

| Target type | Core rule |
|---|---|
| Presence target | 1 for a supported matching feature; 0 for no supported presence in an adequately assessed target; unresolved calls receive a scenario interval. |
| Compatible positive-abundance target with explicit threshold \(L_j>0\) | \(\min(1,x_{dj}/L_j)\), using only the same assay/units/reference. This is a graded supply index, not a dose calculation. |
| Desired range \([L_j,U_j]\) | Supply sufficiency is capped at 1; exceeding \(U_j\) creates a separate excess/context flag. Do not award unlimited benefit for excess. |
| Pathway target | Use complete-route/alternative-route evidence for a complete-pathway goal. A separately declared partial/terminal-gene goal can use its actual measured panel under a frozen rule, without claiming whole-route completeness. |
| Metabolite target | Compare actual compatible assays; never substitute a gene percentile for concentration. |
| Model-only target | Exclude from core measured coverage and expose in the relevant simulation/prediction module. |

Store `supported_value`, `scenario_lower`, `scenario_upper`, rule ID, observation IDs and uncertainty reasons. For an unassessed feature use null supported value and bounds [0,1]. For a questionable trace call use its supported or provisional value plus [0,1] sensitivity as appropriate. These are **scenario bounds**, not a 95% confidence interval.

A well-assessed nondetection has no supported supply contribution. If analytical detection limits leave plausible hidden carriage, record that separately and include an uncertainty scenario; do not label biological absence with certainty. The PDFs' uncalibrated detection powers must not narrow those bounds.

For arithmetic only, define `supported_contribution = supported_value` when known, otherwise 0: no evidence receives no supported credit. The original unknown measurement and `supported_value` remain null, with [0,1] scenario bounds and an unassessed flag. This is an evidence-scoring convention, not conversion of an unknown measurement into observed absence.

For a nonempty set of donors \(S\), the availability ceiling uses those numeric supported contributions:

\[
A_j(S)=\max_{d\in S} a_{dj}.
\]

Define \(A_j(\varnothing)=0\) and \(C(\varnothing)=0\) for marginal calculations; an empty set is never a donor candidate. This counts each target once. It answers whether at least one source supports the target. It is **not a pooled concentration, final community composition, strain-engraftment probability or persistent-restoration forecast**.

Partition the scenario-eligible scoring targets into nonoverlapping goal groups \(G\). Within each group, \(\beta_j\ge0\) sums to 1; group weights \(\alpha_g\ge0\) sum to 1:

\[
C(S)=100\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_g\sum_{j\in g}\beta_j A_j(S).
\]

Default: equal group weights, then equal weights within each group. These are transparent engineering defaults, not published efficacy coefficients. A butyrate goal may have a taxonomic subview and a pathway subview, but their total credit remains capped at that goal's weight. Adding ten redundant butyrate taxa must not multiply the importance of butyrate by ten relative to every other goal. Allow versioned user priority overrides and rerun sensitivity.

For set assessment accounting, a target is fully assessed for its availability ceiling if all included materials were assessed for that target, or one supported member already reaches the capped maximum of 1. Otherwise retain unresolved set assessment weight. This does not alter each material's individual assay gaps or any screening status.

A target cannot belong to two active counting groups. A duplicated observation in several visual panels retains one underlying contribution. Report separate taxa/function matrices even when their combined goal contribution is capped.

For uncertainty bounds, replace each \(a_{dj}\) with its lower/upper value and repeat the same calculation. Unknowns remain in the fixed denominator. Also show `assessed_goal_weight`, `supported_goal_weight`, `unassessed_goal_weight` and the fully enumerated goal list. Never inflate a sparse donor's score by dropping its unmeasured targets from the denominator.

Validate every graded supply threshold as finite and strictly positive. A zero/negative lower bound cannot be used in a division-based supply rule; use a presence, monitoring or explicitly modeled range endpoint instead.

Do not call \(C\) “percent microbiome restored,” “compatibility probability,” or “clinical match accuracy.” Label it **Supported target coverage, 0–100**, with the exact target denominator and whether the figure is report-derived, native-measurement-derived or scenario-based.

### 7.3 Recipient gaps and donor context remain separate

The default coverage formula does not multiply by an invented probability of deficiency. Define scenario inclusion precisely: `reference_only` includes `active` or `conditional` records with `origin=reference_supported` and a compatible reference; `research_goals` includes every `active` or `conditional` record. Both exclude `context_only` and `invalid`. Positive coverage additionally includes only `supply` or `support_range` goals with an implemented core scoring rule; `avoid_introduction`, `monitor` and model-only goals stay in separate comparison panels, outside its benefit denominator. The default displayed ranking uses `research_goals`; also show `reference_only` when nonempty. Freeze each scenario denominator across all candidates. These scenarios mean reference-supported goals only, and all active research goals including conditional hypotheses. Uncertain goals remain visible with their own sensitivity. If no reference-supported goals exist, render the research-goal scenario and explicitly identify it. If a scenario has no eligible positive-weight scoring goals, return `coverage=null`, `reason=no_active_scoring_targets`, and no goal-based rank. Still render descriptive donor/concern/model comparisons. This differs from a nonempty goal set with zero supported coverage.

Increased relative abundance after hypothetical addition can result from denominator changes. Do not assume the recipient has an absolute deficiency from a low percentage. Absolute-load inputs can improve interpretation but are optional.

A donor with lower measured abundance can still supply a relevant strain. A donor with a higher abundance can fail to engraft. The core score compares supply evidence; the prediction module handles a different endpoint.

### 7.4 Distance, overlap and diversity descriptors

Compute, when input compatibility allows:

- Bray–Curtis dissimilarity on the same nonnegative feature universe and denominator.
- Jaccard overlap using a frozen detection rule, with depth sensitivity.
- Aitchison/CLR distance only with a frozen feature set and documented zero handling; do not fit the feature universe from the current winning candidate.
- Donor/recipient Shannon values, ratio where the denominator is positive, species richness and evenness, with analytical comparability flags.
- Target-specific overlap, donor-unique supported targets, recipient-persistent strain overlap, taxon/pathway redundancy and unresolved strain fraction.
- Genome/model coverage and functional redundancy where the underlying data support them.

These descriptors do not receive universal positive/negative weights. Fit indication-specific effects in a trained model or display them. In particular, the two signs of donor–recipient distance cannot both be simplified to a universal biological rule. A high-diversity donor can supply unwanted organisms; a low-diversity donor can supply a useful missing strain.

### 7.5 Default ranking without fake precision

Return all individual candidates and their contributions, plus separate views:

1. `conditional_research_ranking`: analytically computable candidates with no confirmed exclusion. Missing screening and unresolved review findings remain prominent alongside each row.
2. `excluded_candidates`: confirmed exclusions with full analytical results, exact reasons and scope. Excluded material cannot appear in a proposed set.
3. `not_computable_candidates`: no usable common features or identity/QC failure, with exact missing inputs. This is not a biological exclusion of the person.

Within the conditional view, compute Pareto fronts using this common objective vector: lower scenario coverage, supported coverage and assessed goal weight (maximize); scenario width, unique donor count and material count (minimize). Singletons have both counts one. Compare only candidates within the same goal scenario and compatible analytical scope. A candidate dominates another only if every objective is no worse and at least one is better. Numerical tolerance is a documented floating-point tolerance, not a clinical difference threshold.

Hazards are not reduced to a weighted risk count in this default vector. Show exact concern identities, analytical coverage and review status alongside the restoration ranking. Unknown hazard coverage cannot become an advantage. A confirmed exclusion removes material from admissible sets; an unresolved concern stays visible independently of benefit. Call these Pareto fronts restoration/objective fronts, not overall safety-optimal fronts. Keep concern-distinct alternatives accessible even if dominated on restoration metrics. A separate user-declared concern-stratified view may compare candidates with equivalent assessed hazard classes, but must not imply calibrated risk.

For deterministic display order use: Pareto front; lower scenario coverage descending; supported coverage descending; assessed goal weight descending; unique donor count ascending, then material count ascending; stable candidate ID. Label tied or sensitivity-unstable alternatives as such. Do not convert the ordering into “safest” or “best clinical donor.” A user may choose a declared goal-priority scenario and see how the order changes.

Clinical-screening completeness is displayed as a separate axis, not an arithmetic benefit that can compensate for a positive hazard. An unresolved pathogen review is never hidden because the donor ranks first for butyrate coverage.

## 8. Transmission concerns and evidence-based exclusions

### 8.1 Four distinct kinds of evidence

1. **Confirmed hazard or a documented applicable program criterion:** may exclude material from the admissible set search; preserve the profile and exact rule.
2. **Credible unresolved high-consequence signal:** retain conditional research comparison, label review pending and identify confirmation needed. Never represent it as a negative screen.
3. **Mechanistic concern:** source-qualified animal, cellular, genetic or biochemical evidence; display separately without inventing a human FMT risk percentage.
4. **Disease association:** contextual community resemblance, with overlapping features and confounding. It does not itself establish a transmissible causal entity.

Never let a high restoration score cancel an exclusion or erase a concern. Conversely, do not exclude for vague dysbiosis, a generic “bad organism” name, an uncalibrated disease percentile, any ARG, the number of AMR families or a arbitrary relative-abundance cutoff.

### 8.2 Embedded rule registry

Each rule below compiles to a record with rule ID, condition, required evidence, disposition, scope and source. Rules only fire on the stated evidence. They do not authorize a new universal shotgun cutoff.

| Rule | Trigger and evidence requirement | Disposition | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| X01 | Validated, material-linked confirmation of a qualifying transmissible enteric pathogen | Exclude the affected material from admissible sets under the applicable screening policy; preserve research measurements. | Pathogen-specific confirmation and a named current program rule; FDA events below establish why this is consequential. |
| X02 | Confirmed qualifying ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae, CRE, VRE or MRSA under applicable screening criteria | Exclude affected material. A taxon plus an unlinked resistance fragment is insufficient. | FDA's 2019 MDRO screening requirements, S01. |
| X03 | Confirmed specimen identity mix-up, wrong person, wrong material or failed analytical integrity | Exclude that analytical/material record from set search; request the correct input. Do not permanently exclude the person. | Identity is a necessary computational condition, not a disease hypothesis. |
| X04 | Explicit documented clinical-program criterion is met, with the criterion's source/version and evidence | Separate `program_exclusion` from demonstrated microbial hazard; show criterion and applicability. | Imported named program rules; do not invent an age/BMI cutoff from an abstract. |
| R01 | Credible but unconfirmed pathogen/pathotype/toxin signal | High-priority review; conditional comparisons continue. A set containing it inherits the unresolved concern. | Analytical uncertainty and S01–S03. |
| R02 | ARG/mobile element without resolved carrier, intact allele, context or phenotype | Evidence review, not automatic MDRO exclusion. | Organism-specific confirmation differs from family-level sequence detection. |
| R03 | Credible pks/clb island or bft/ETBF-related evidence, without confirmed clinical pathogen classification | Prioritized mechanistic/toxin review. An explicit precautionary program rule can be represented separately. | S07–S08; no validated human FMT cancer-risk threshold. |

| C02 | Confirmed common colonizer without relevant pathogenic determinant/context | Context-dependent review if warranted; no species-name-only exclusion. | Strain, host and site matter. |
| C03 | Noninfectious animal-transfer or mechanistic evidence | Mechanistic annotation with exact model; no automatic human disease-transfer conclusion. | S06–S10. |

`X01` is a rule template, not an executable list of every pathogen cutoff. Instantiate it using the existing v05 target registry, validated external test results and the named screening policy. If that policy is not provided, a confirmed medically consequential transmissible pathogen can be explicitly excluded on hazard evidence with a documented source; do not pretend a comprehensive clinical program screen was performed. Ambiguous metagenomic flags cannot be auto-promoted to that confirmation state.

Every exclusion record must include the source finding, rule, date, scope, resolution and reason in one sentence understandable without code. Example: “This donation is excluded from candidate sets because a linked validated test confirmed ESBL-producing E. coli; rule X02 applies.” Do not output “excluded: safety” alone.

### 8.4 Set concerns are a union, not an average

A set inherits every member's confirmed exclusion and unresolved concern. Exclude every set containing confirmed-excluded material. Adding a donor cannot dilute away an exclusion. Deduplicate repeated evidence for the same hazard while retaining member-specific origins and assay coverage.

Do not compute `1 − product(1 − donor_risk)` without known calibrated risks and dependence assumptions. Do not divide hazard abundance by donor count and then pronounce a set safe. Shared source/household, batch and relatedness can correlate both hazards and strain contributions.

The report must show “No confirmed exclusion identified in the available evidence” instead of “No disease-transfer risk.” Required screening not covered by the assay remains a visible list, including blood-borne/systemic agents and targets outside ordinary stool DNA detection. The matcher is not a replacement for the program's medical history, examination and validated laboratory screening.

## 9. Donor-set optimization

### 9.1 Search objective and candidate universe

Represent a donor candidate as a **person × donation/material unit**, not just a name. Multiple samples from the same donor are replicates or longitudinal evidence unless explicitly linked to distinct material units. Do not count replicate samples as extra donors or extra diversity.

For every admissible singleton and candidate set, calculate:

- Supported availability ceiling and lower/upper scenarios, by target and goal group.
- Measured taxa/function coverage, assessed fraction and unresolved targets.
- Incremental coverage versus the best singleton evaluated under the same objective.
- Unique targets contributed by each member and leave-one-member-out marginal gain.
- Redundant coverage, strain overlap and likely attribution ambiguity.
- Qualified concern union, with newly introduced concerns by member.
- Model outputs supported for that exposure type; simulation outputs separately.
- Search completeness, model/reference applicability and rank stability.

No universal maximum donor count is supported here. Store both `material_count` and `unique_donor_count`. The user flag `--max-donors-per-set` limits unique people; an optional `--max-materials-per-set` limits distinct material units. Both counts enter parsimony/Pareto comparisons so redundant lots cannot evade the size objective. Default to parsimony when an added member contributes no supported benefit or adds only redundancy; this is an engineering tie-break, not proof that one donor is always safer. Report when an added donor offers a tradeoff rather than an improvement.

For a set \(S\), define marginal supported coverage:

\[
\Delta_d(S)=C(S)-C(S\setminus\{d\}),\quad
\Delta_{single}(S)=C(S)-\max_{d\in S}C(\{d\}).
\]

Also compare with the best singleton in the whole candidate list, not only members of that set. State which comparator is used. The target denominator and policy must remain identical across those comparisons.

### 9.2 Do not mistake the availability ceiling for a pool forecast

Show three distinct views:

1. **Source availability:** the max/union coverage above. It is the optimistic list of supported source features.
2. **Contribution sensitivity:** stress-test reduced representation or loss of each donor's distinctive features. This is a hypothetical computational scenario, not a physical mixture recipe.
3. **Ecological prediction:** only a model trained/validated for set-valued exposure may produce a corresponding calibrated prediction. A single-donor model on a synthetic union is labeled out-of-domain simulation.

If users request a virtual abundance mixture, accept explicitly supplied abstract contribution weights \(w_d\ge0,\sum w_d=1\) and compute \(x_j^{virtual}=\sum_d w_dx_{dj}\) only for compatible units. Default scenario weights can be equal for exploration, with conspicuous labeling. They represent relative analytical contributions, **not stool mass, viable-cell quantities, capsule counts or recommended mixing proportions**. Do not include them in the clinical-facing recommendation or convert them to preparation instructions.

Different organisms can lose viability, inhibit one another, share resources, replace resident strains or bloom after combination. Neither adding relative abundance vectors nor averaging donor percentiles accounts for those effects. Show unique supply, redundancy and modeled interactions separately.

The independence formula \(1-\prod_d(1-p_{dj})\) is not a default pooled-engraftment probability. It assumes independent opportunities and overlooks shared recipient resistance, competition and dilution. If exposed for sensitivity research, label it an independence scenario and do not call it calibrated.

### 9.3 Enumeration and scalable search

Let \(N\) be analytically usable, non-excluded material candidates. Include all singletons. The unconstrained material-subset count is \(2^N-1\). With a maximum of K material units and no other constraint, the count is \(\sum_{k=1}^{\min(K,N)}\binom Nk\). A unique-person limit with several materials per person requires an exact grouped count/enumeration; do not substitute the material-count formula. Report unconstrained and constraint-feasible counts separately. The budget includes mandatory singletons; if it is smaller than N, raise the effective budget to N and record that adjustment, so every individual remains evaluated. When the feasible count fits the effective budget, enumerate all feasible nonempty subsets. For larger spaces:

1. Memoize analytically identical calculations only when every input used by that objective is equivalent. Preserve candidate/material identities, metadata, concerns and membership alternatives; do not delete a donor merely because its species table matches another donor.
2. Use deterministic branch-and-bound with a valid upper bound for the objective being optimized. Certifying maximum coverage does not certify the complete multi-objective Pareto archive. For a full Pareto certificate, pruning must prove vector dominance; alternatively solve coverage separately by cardinality and label any additional uncertainty archive approximate. Use a budgeted beam search when valid bounds are unavailable.
3. Seed with all singletons and greedily augmented sets, then evaluate local add/drop/swap alternatives.
4. Preserve the §7.5 Pareto archive across coverage, assessment coverage, uncertainty and both material/person counts; attach concern identities separately.
5. Stop at budget and report `search_complete: false`, evaluated counts, skipped regions and whether any optimality certificate exists.

For the binary-coverage surrogate, an optional mixed-integer formulation is:

\[
\max \sum_j w_j z_j;\qquad z_j\le\sum_d a_{dj}y_d;\quad y_d,z_j\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Here binary \(a_{dj}\) means supported supply; enforce user constraints and exclusion masks explicitly. If donor count is constrained, expose that constraint in the request. Without a count constraint, monotone union coverage naturally favors including many donors; the Pareto frontier and marginal-gain report are therefore essential. Do not claim the set-cover objective proves ecological optimality.

For graded coverage use auxiliary per-target maximum/assignment variables or evaluate exact set values; do not incorrectly reuse the binary linear constraints. Risk/competition terms require separate empirical models and cannot be invented just to force a smaller answer.

### 9.4 Deterministic core pseudocode

```python
def match(request, adapters, rules, model_registry):
    materials = ingest_and_validate(request, adapters)
    recipient = select_recipient_baseline(materials, request)
    observations = harmonize_without_silent_unit_conversion(materials)
    features = derive_or_reuse_features(observations, request)
    targets = build_targets_once(recipient, request, features)
    exclusions, concerns, screening = evaluate_qualified_evidence(materials, rules)

    candidates = []
    for material in donor_material_units(materials):
        candidate = compare_singleton(recipient, material, targets, features)
        candidate.attach(exclusions, concerns, screening)
        candidate.predictions = run_compatible_models_or_record_gaps(
            recipient, [material], model_registry, features
        )
        candidates.append(candidate)

    searchable = [c for c in candidates
                  if c.analysis_status != "not_computable"
                  and c.exclusion_status != "excluded_on_confirmed_evidence"]
    sets, search_audit = enumerate_or_search(searchable, request.search)
    results = []
    for members in sets:
        result = coverage_with_fixed_denominator(members, targets)
        result.concerns = qualified_union(members)
        result.marginals = leave_one_member_out(members, targets)
        result.interactions = ecological_descriptors(members, recipient, features)
        result.predictions = exposure_compatible_predictions(members, recipient)
        results.append(result)

    scenarios = evaluate_declared_sensitivity(candidates, results, targets, request)
    ranking = pareto_and_deterministic_display_order(candidates, results, scenarios)
    output = canonical_output(ranking, exclusions, concerns, screening,
                              targets, search_audit, scenarios)
    validate_lineage_and_claim_classes(output)
    render_all_formats_from_json(output)
    return output
```

No exception handler may silently replace a failed advanced model with its last cached prediction from another recipient or rename a heuristic as that model. Fallback outputs identify their actual method.

## 10. Predictive models: implementable recipes and adapters

### 10.1 Public-data baseline that the agent must build

Implement `public-strain-baseline-v1` using the public resources in §14, starting with data whose donor/recipient/time mapping is actually available. Do not wait for MOZAIC's absent arrays to build a useful predictor.

One training row is recipient baseline × actually assigned donor/material × evaluable taxon/strain × prespecified follow-up window. Required predictors, all available before exposure:

- Donor and recipient abundance, observation status and callable support.
- Donor-specific versus shared strain evidence; baseline recipient occupancy and ambiguity.
- Species prevalence calculated in training data only, with a frozen prior for unseen taxa.
- Same-lane community distance, diversity and target/group features.
- Known indication, exposure type, prior antibiotics/conditioning, route and follow-up horizon when recorded; unknown stays explicit. These are training context variables, not instructions to change treatment.
- Optional functional complementarity, metabolic descriptors and model-coverage values.

Baseline models: prevalence-only; recipient-only; donor-only; pairwise regularized logistic regression; then random forest or gradient boosting if external evaluation supports improvement. Use class weighting only within training folds and retain the true calibration prevalence. A hierarchical taxon/study model is appropriate where effective independent samples are limited.

Primary endpoint: donor-specific strain detection in a fixed window, among evaluable source/baseline comparisons. Secondary endpoints: persistent detection at later windows, recipient strain retention, post-FMT species presence and community-composition error. Define windows in the training recipe from actual available data; do not pool a day-seven and month-six outcome as the same event. Predefine one primary window per model artifact and retain exact observation times.

Ambiguous source attribution and insufficient depth are `unevaluable` labels, not negative examples. Keep their frequency visible. Do not train an apparently accurate classifier by excluding difficult cases and then claim it applies to all taxa.

Executable donor-strain label rules:

| Donor source / baseline / follow-up evidence | Label |
|---|---|
| Adequate donor-specific call; adequate baseline discrimination supports non-detection of that source strain; adequate follow-up detects source-specific evidence | 1, donor-specific establishment in the defined window. |
| Same adequate source/baseline conditions; follow-up has validated target-specific ascertainment and does not detect source-specific evidence | 0 for detection in that window, with assay limitation retained. |
| Donor and baseline share an indistinguishable strain, multiple sources cannot be resolved, or any required ascertainment is inadequate | Unevaluable/ambiguous; not a negative training row. |
| Follow-up target not assessed or insufficiently sensitive | Unevaluable, even if donor and baseline are well characterized. |

Adequate ascertainment of absence does not require an actual strain call in the recipient. Define it using validated target-specific reference/depth/marker detection performance; otherwise true establishment and true negative rows would be incorrectly removed. A genus-level or species-level signal alone cannot satisfy donor-specific identity.

### 10.6 Let advanced algorithms produce their own useful ranking views

Advanced algorithms must influence an explicit objective view, not merely decorate a coverage-only report. Implement `--objective measured_coverage | predicted_target_attainment | model_endpoint | mechanistic_target_pareto`, with model, endpoint, horizon and scenario flags where required. Default remains measured coverage because it is widely computable. The report retains all views actually available and explains why their leading candidates may differ.

`ObjectiveView` requires: view ID; endpoint; horizon; frozen target manifest and weights; compatible exposures; model/scenario IDs; applicability per candidate; objective vector; denominator; search algorithm; search completeness; and ranking explanation. Unsupported candidates are unranked only in that view, while their core comparisons remain available.

For a calibrated binary target event at a specified endpoint \(e\) and time \(t\), define:

\[
T_{e,t}(d)=100\sum_{j\in J_{e,t}}w_j P_\theta(Y_{j,e,t}=1\mid R,d),\qquad \sum_jw_j=1.
\]

This is **expected weighted target attainment**, not a clinical-response probability. Compile \(J_{e,t}\) from recipient goals and the model's frozen endpoint domain before comparing candidates; report goals outside that domain separately. Linearity of expectation does not require independent target events. However, a grouped event such as “at least one strain in this set establishes” needs its own joint model; do not derive it by assuming independent marginal events.

Missing predictions stay in the denominator. If \(K\) is the set with available predictions, completion bounds are:

\[
T_{min}=100\sum_{j\in K}w_jp_j,\qquad
T_{max}=T_{min}+100\sum_{j\notin K}w_j.
\]

Return full expected attainment as null when it is not fully predicted; show available-prediction contribution, predictive coverage and completion bounds. These are missing-data bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. Rank compatible candidates on declared attainment/bounds, predictive coverage and uncertainty with the §7.5 count dimensions. Distinguish complete estimates from partial bounds.

A species-presence model cannot populate donor-strain-establishment targets. A point abundance regression cannot supply a probability of reaching an abundance range without a validated predictive distribution. MOZAIC convergence and any clinical response output retain their own `model_endpoint` views; neither becomes generic target attainment. Set-valued probability views require models validated for their corresponding exposure type.

For a mechanistic view, simulate recipient baseline and the hypothetical candidate-associated community under the same declared scenario \(s\). Define a frozen target-directed or range-based loss \(\ell_j\):

\[
\Delta_{j,s}(d)=\ell_j(F_{R,j,s})-\ell_j(F_{R+d,j,s}).
\]

Positive means movement toward the specified modeled target; negative means movement away. Preserve both signs. Use explicit units or fixed reference scales, never candidate-batch extrema. Construct Pareto views across target-specific improvements, represented model coverage, scenario variation and donor/material counts. Worst-case axes may use \(\min_s\Delta_{j,s}\), labeled as worst case over the declared scenarios, not over all possible biology. Do not average unrelated fluxes and clinical/convergence outcomes into one score.

A simulator producing standalone donor descriptors cannot claim a recipient improvement; it stays a descriptor view until a joint-community scenario is implemented. Hypothetical donor sets may be ranked in a mechanistic view with their explicit analytical contribution assumptions and uncertainty, never as calibrated physical-pool outcomes.

Search each selected objective separately, then compare the union of evaluated alternatives. Coverage-based pruning is invalid for potentially nonmonotone empirical or mechanistic objectives unless an objective-specific bound is proven. With limited compute, label the archive approximate. The report may say one donor leads measured coverage while another leads a supported prediction or simulation view, but only when those computations were actually performed.

## 11. Mechanistic and ecological simulation

### 11.1 Implementable independent MICOM layer

Implement `metabolic-complementarity-v1` as an explicitly independent research model, not a claim of exact Zhang replay. Pin MICOM, metabolic model collection, mapping table, medium, solver and all constraints. Use actual donor/recipient abundances where compatible. Record modeled taxon count, represented abundance, excluded taxa, unmapped pathways and infeasible samples.

For each recipient and donor, simulate exchange profiles across a small declared set of plausible media. A default medium is a modeling assumption, not the participant's actual diet. If diet is supplied, map it through a versioned conversion and retain uncertainty. Do not optimize a diet or conditioning treatment in this release.

Derive interpretable descriptors: overlap in resource demands, modeled metabolite production/consumption, potential donor–recipient cross-feeding, niche novelty, and target-pathway coverage. Model secondary bile acids and other transformations only when the reaction/model collection actually represents them. An absent reaction in the database is model absence, not biological absence.

Output simulated flux units and constraints. Do not label a gene-based flux as measured butyrate, TMAO, histamine or host exposure. Community metabolic models can produce different results under alternative growth tradeoffs and media; report sensitivity rather than one precise expected concentration.

### 11.2 Reproducing the published metabolic features

For exact repository replay, the observed source code separates genome-length normalization for MICOM from pseudocount/median-total normalization for the regression. Preserve that distinction. The distance feature uses aligned metabolite profiles and a Ledoit–Wolf precision estimate; it then summarizes nearest-neighbor distances. `met_independence` comes from an anvi'o workflow. Inspect each pinned helper rather than substituting a generic cosine distance with the same field name.

Freeze training normalization references. Recomputing a median scale from whatever donors happen to be submitted can make one pair's prediction change when an unrelated candidate is added. Such candidate-batch dependence fails inference reproducibility unless it is an explicitly declared joint-set model.

### 11.3 Co-engraftment modules and competition

Build module features from training-only co-engraftment data or a frozen published module manifest. For an independent implementation, create an evaluable taxon-by-exposure label matrix; estimate co-establishment associations within study/time strata with prevalence and baseline occupancy accounted for; cluster the resulting association graph; choose regularization/cluster cut inside grouped inner validation; and freeze memberships before outer testing. Report cluster stability and do not encode missing labels as failures. This is a new model recipe, not a claimed reproduction of unpublished module coefficients. For each donor/set, report supported module members, missing members, module uncertainty and donor-source overlap. A module is a statistical association until a causal interaction is experimentally supported.

Do not impose universal “same species cannot coexist” or “all donor strains add” rules. Use strain resolution, species-specific occupancy and empirical model effects. Where available, include strain carrying-capacity and baseline priority-effect descriptors, with model/source scope. If a module or taxon is absent from the reference database, return unavailable rather than a zero interaction.

Optional SMETANA or comparable model outputs can annotate potential metabolic dependencies. They do not establish actual cross-feeding in a recipient. Validate whether those features improve held-out predictions beyond abundance and prevalence baselines before increasing their role in an empirical ranking model.

### 11.4 Host compatibility cannot be fully inferred from stool DNA

Store optional actual GMLR/immune assay results, allergies/clinical history and relevant medications in separate typed modalities. A model may use them only if it was trained with equivalent inputs. Missing host data widens applicability uncertainty; it does not disable basic microbial comparison. Never impute “no immune reaction” from missing assays.

## 12. Uncertainty, validation and learning from follow-up

### 12.1 Four uncertainty classes

- **Analytical:** depth, reference coverage, identity ambiguity, contamination controls, detection limits and quantitative units.
- **Goal/reference:** whether a low feature is meaningful for this recipient and whether the chosen desired state is justified.
- **Model:** fitted-parameter variation, calibration, domain transport, endpoint and time horizon.
- **Ecological/material:** strain competition, shared host constraints, viability, donation differences and nonlinear pool behavior.

Report these separately. Do not collapse them into an arbitrary “confidence 93%.” A bootstrap rank frequency is not the chance a donor will work clinically. A scenario envelope is not a statistical confidence interval.

### 12.2 Required sensitivity runs

Core matching: active research goals versus reference-supported goals; equal versus declared user weights; trace calls included versus unresolved; unassessed feature bounds; comparable alternative reference bundles; donor lot/replicate consistency; shared-source/namespace discordance.

Advanced models: repeated grouped training seeds where relevant; depth/strain-threshold sensitivity; alternative media and model coverage; preprocessing replay versus corrected retrain; horizon variation only within trained support. Do not create a simulated 95% interval by assigning arbitrary distributions to unknown inputs.

Summarize ranking stability as the proportion of **declared sensitivity scenarios** in which a candidate belongs to the leading Pareto set, with scenario count and definitions. For approximate search, search each scenario and reevaluate the union archive where practical; otherwise label frequencies explicitly as conditional on the evaluated archive. Neither approach proves global stability outside an incomplete search. If using a statistical bootstrap, state the resampling unit and interval method separately.

### 12.3 Leakage-resistant evaluation

Use post-FMT data only for outcomes/label ascertainment. No post-FMT abundance, response-dependent time selection, future sample-based feature filtering or post-treatment clinical label may enter baseline prediction features. If post-FMT MAGs help determine strain labels, isolate that reference as label-only and do not use it to improve baseline predictors retrospectively.

Outer validation: leave one study out, then untouched external cohorts when available. Also report unseen-recipient/known-donor, unseen-donor and unseen-indication performance separately. For a claim covering unseen donor and recipient, split connected components of the donor–recipient graph. Random taxon rows, reads or repeated samples are not independent validation units.

Inner validation: grouped hyperparameter selection, feature selection, imputation and calibration in training data only. All visits and replicates from the same participant/material lineage stay together. Deduplicate historical cohorts reused by different meta-analyses; a new publication does not make an old sample independent.

Required metrics: AUROC and precision–recall performance for binary endpoints; Brier/log loss and calibration plot/intercept/slope; composition error for regression; per-study/indication/time/assay results; evaluable coverage; subgroup uncertainty; and performance relative to simple baselines. Brier score alone is not a pure calibration metric. Bootstrap independent study/person/donor units, not species rows.

Do not promote a model merely because its AUROC exceeds an arbitrary threshold. Promotion requires a correctly defined endpoint, valid splits, usable coverage, calibration appropriate to its claim and evidence that it adds value over simpler alternatives in the intended domain. If a model is exploratory, keep it available with that label rather than deleting it.

### 12.4 Matching-policy validation differs from outcome prediction

Most datasets observe a recipient with the donor actually assigned, not every alternative donor. Good factual prediction does not identify which unobserved donor would have worked best. Retrospective simulated selection must remain labeled as such. Evaluate donor-selection benefit only under a design that identifies that question, such as prospective randomized allocation or a defensible documented causal design with its assumptions.

Likewise, arbitrary in-silico removal/addition of donors is not a real pooled experiment. Use actual multi-donor membership/exposure records to test set models. Report pool identity, donor order where applicable, protocol and sequencing depth; do not infer pool membership solely from the model you are validating.

### 12.5 Follow-up command

Given actual follow-up data, report source-attributed strain persistence, recipient strain retention/replacement, target coverage changes, functional changes and independently measured clinical outcomes. Preserve time points and material identities. A compositional change toward a donor must not automatically be labeled symptom improvement.

Include unrelated-donor controls and shared-strain ambiguity. Separate actual assigned donors from candidates merely evaluated before exposure. Never claim that an unadministered donor “engrafted” because it shares a common strain with the follow-up sample.

Learning from a small personal sample series is allowed as an explicitly personal longitudinal observation. It must not automatically retrain the public clinical model, establish general safety or use donor self-reported health as ground-truth absence of disease.

## 13. Report and output design

### 13.1 Front-loaded report order

The main report must answer the matching question before long background explanations. Use the existing report visual language, but do not repeat hundreds of pages of unchanged donor reports.

1. **Recipient goals and comparison summary:** recipient ID, donor/material IDs, data completeness, active goal scenario, one-sentence interpretation, and explicit research status.
2. **All individual donors:** side-by-side coverage, distinctive contributions, assessed fraction, unresolved targets, concern status and model applicability. Do not hide lower-ranked or excluded donors.
3. **Best alternative sets and best-singleton comparison:** donor membership, marginal supported gain, redundancy, each member's unique contribution, inherited/new concerns and prediction/simulation status.
4. **Recipient-target matrix:** every active and context-only target; recipient observation; each donor observation; set coverage; units; provenance; reason for inclusion.
5. **Transmission concerns and screening gaps:** specific findings, analytical strength, causal tier, confirmed exclusions if any, unresolved flags and absent tests. This must be visible near the front, not buried after benefit graphics.
6. **Engraftment and ecology:** what models can predict, their population/time endpoint, strain overlap, shared-source ambiguity and functional/metabolic scenarios.
7. **Sensitivity and alternative goal priorities:** stable choices, tradeoffs, ties and goals that drive changes.
8. **Detailed evidence and provenance:** complete observations, source links, model/data versions, search completeness and limitations.

For many donors, show an overview matrix and paginate complete individual/set dossiers. Never silently truncate all candidates to the first eight or all goals to a top-five list. HTML can filter/search; PDF includes an index and the complete evaluated Pareto set plus a searchable appendix/summary of other evaluated sets. Avoid printing millions of subsets: include a complete machine-readable set table or compact indexed artifact in the run outputs and explicitly report evaluated/search counts. This is an application output, not a missing supplemental specification.

### 13.2 Visual encoding

Use aligned bar lengths for supported coverage and a lighter interval band for scenario bounds. Label the scale as an index. Show the denominator and assessed percentage directly beneath it. Use distinct icons/text for measured, predicted, simulated and unresolved; color alone is insufficient.

The set comparison should use a target-contribution matrix or stacked contribution chart with shared coverage counted once. A second column must show marginal coverage beyond the best singleton. Zero marginal gain is printed as zero, not an enthusiastic “combined super-donor” claim.

Display concern evidence with categorical labels: confirmed exclusion; review pending; context; not assessed. Avoid green shields, infection-free percentages or a combined benefit/safety speedometer. A high-function donor with an unresolved pathogen signal must visibly retain that signal on its summary card.

Use plain labels: “What this donor could supply,” “What remains unresolved,” “What the combination adds,” “Predicted persistence” only when available, and “Screening records.” Reserve detailed algorithm jargon for drilldowns.

## 17. Evidence review scope and completion criteria

This review covered the supplied primary papers and OpenBiome repositories; large cross-cohort engraftment studies; species/strain attribution; community and metabolic matching; physical pools versus non-pooled/sequential donor exposure; human transmission events and causal mechanisms; Long COVID/ME/CFS applicability; public datasets, code licenses and reproducibility defects; and the actual three report PDFs. Searches and primary-source checks extended through 10 September 2026, including journal publications from September 2 and September 7. PubMed/Europe PMC, publisher/PMC articles, official FDA pages, trial registries, author repositories and public data archives were used.

This is a broad, implementation-focused review, not a claim that every publication or unindexed dataset in existence has been found. Exact full-text/artifact gaps are recorded above. Do not fill them with invented coefficients, thresholds, model weights, screening rules or performance figures. The core algorithms here are deliberately complete enough to run without those gaps being resolved.

The build is complete when an agent can point the CLI at one recipient and multiple donor data bundles, obtain a reproducible comparison of every donor and useful donor sets, inspect exactly what each supplies and what concerns remain, see the contribution of every algorithm, and reproduce the output from its manifest. Confirmed exclusions must be specific and auditable; uncertainty must be visible; extra donors must justify their incremental contribution; and the tool must never confuse a promising research match with demonstrated safe, effective FMT.
