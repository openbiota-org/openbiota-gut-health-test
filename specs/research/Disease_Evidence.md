# Gut microbiome transfer and disease: experimental evidence catalogue

**Research cutoff: 11 September 2026.** Human, human-to-animal and animal-to-animal evidence. All references and interpretation are contained in this document. The catalogue includes more than 200 research references, including positive experiments, negative results, methodological analyses and explicitly labeled boundary cases; that is not a count of 200 successful disease transfers.

## The answer

**Yes. Gut-community transfer has caused measurable disease-related features, or increased disease susceptibility/severity, across many organ systems in controlled experiments.** Examples include altered adiposity and glucose regulation; intestinal pain and motility; autoimmune inflammation; cognitive, motor and behavioral changes; skin reactivity; lung inflammation; reproductive dysfunction; and promotion of tumors in susceptible recipients. These findings go beyond simple microbiome–disease correlations.

The strongest defensible statement is usually **“this donor community contributed causally to these measured recipient features under these conditions.”** It is often stronger than association, but narrower than saying the recipient acquired the entire human disease. A mouse can develop impaired memory without having established human Alzheimer's disease; a cancer-prone mouse can develop larger tumors without feces initiating cancer in a normal host.

This is a broad primary-source evidence catalogue, not a registered systematic review or a guarantee that every published experiment has been captured. Negative findings, replications, corrections, preprints and commonly confused examples are retained. A study can be scientifically useful even when it is small or uses animals; its actual endpoint and controls determine what it supports.

## How to read the catalogue

| Evidence pattern | What counts | How to interpret it |
|---|---|---|
| **Feature induction** | A disease-associated community produces a new symptom, physiological defect or pathology relative to control-community recipients. | Direct evidence for a transferable contribution to that feature. Germ-free or antibiotic-conditioned recipients can still be essential. |
| **Susceptibility or worsening** | The community increases severity after a genetic, dietary, immune, chemical, injury or tumor challenge. | A transferable disease modifier; the microbiome was not necessarily sufficient alone. |
| **Loss of protection** | Healthy-community recipients are protected, while disease-community recipients resemble germ-free or vehicle controls. | Supports missing protective functions; does not establish that the disease community actively induces pathology. |
| **Molecular component only** | Inflammation, antibodies, metabolites or gene expression change without demonstrated clinical-type features. | Mechanistic evidence, with a narrower endpoint. |
| **Rescue only** | Healthy FMT or another microbial intervention improves an already diseased recipient. | Supports modifiability, but does not demonstrate that diseased-donor microbiota transfers the illness. |
| **Related transfer** | Cohousing, maternal transmission, intestinal mucosal material, defined bacteria, metabolites or extracellular vesicles. | Valuable evidence, labeled separately from whole fecal/cecal-community transfer. |
| **Negative or reversed result** | Engraftment occurs without expected pathology, or the healthy donor produces the worse result. | Defines the dependence on donor, recipient and experimental context. |

**Abbreviations:** GF = germ-free; antibiotic-conditioned = resident microbiota experimentally depleted; WT = wild-type, without the specified disease-predisposing transgene; EAE = experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis; DSS/AOM = experimental colitis/carcinogenesis challenges; FMT = fecal microbiota transplantation. These conditioning labels explain the evidence; this document is not a transplantation protocol.

## 1. Long COVID, post-COVID illness and pain

### What the Long COVID papers actually support

There is direct disease-community transfer evidence, including a 2026 study of people with symptoms persisting for two years. The endpoints include intestinal/lung inflammation, anxiety or cognitive changes, and increased responses to secondary challenges. The reviewed experiments do **not** establish transfer of every defining Long COVID feature, such as post-exertional malaise, to recipients. Earlier post-COVID donors were not uniformly a rigorously defined Long COVID cohort. Two newer mechanistic reports remain preprints and may share investigators or donor resources.


### LC01 — Post-COVID inflammatory and cognitive features

**Gut microbiota from patients with COVID-19 cause alterations in mice that resemble post-COVID symptoms.** 2023. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2023.2249146); PMID 37668317.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Previously infected human donors versus control donors → GF mice; healthy/household comparisons and a separate bacterial lung-challenge arm.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Lung inflammatory changes and impaired cognition; more severe illness after a separate Klebsiella pneumoniae challenge. SARS-CoV-2 was not detected in the relevant donor feces/recipient lung assessments.
- **What this establishes:** Human-to-mouse transfer of selected postinfectious features. Donors had prior mild/asymptomatic COVID and were not all established Long COVID cases; the bacterial challenge is additional, not spontaneous infection from the original stool.


### LC02 — Long COVID persisting two years

**Gut Microbiota from Patients with Long COVID Persisting for 2 Years Result in Alterations in Mice that Resemble Post-COVID Symptoms.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12602-026-11197-2); PMID 42667585.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Stool from 11 people with two-year Long COVID versus 11 SARS-CoV-2-unexposed controls → antibiotic-conditioned mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Lung and intestinal inflammation and anxiety-like behavior; worse responses in separate K. pneumoniae infection and stress-related IBS-D models. A Streptococcus salivarius isolate was tested separately.
- **What this establishes:** Peer-reviewed disease-community evidence for selected features. The comparator was unexposed controls, not recovered people without Long COVID, so Long-COVID-specific attribution remains incomplete. No demonstrated transfer of the full fatigue/PEM syndrome. Primary abstract verified; published 29 August 2026.


### LC03 — Long COVID barrier and neuroimmune features — PREPRINT

**Microbiota-derived extracellular vesicles link intestinal dysbiosis to neuroimmune activation in long COVID.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.28.708602).

- **Transfer and comparator:** Neurological Long COVID donor communities → GF mice; separate microbiota-derived extracellular-vesicle experiments in cellular and animal systems.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Intestinal-barrier disruption and neuroimmune activation; vesicle arms implicated inflammasome, macrophage and microglial pathways.
- **What this establishes:** Whole-community and vesicle-only results answer different questions. This is a March 2026 preprint; no peer-reviewed version was located. Investigators/resources may overlap LC04, so do not count them automatically as independent clinical-cohort replications.


### LC04 — Long COVID immune dysregulation — PREPRINT

**Microbiota-induced intestinal barrier disruption drives BAFF-mediated B-cell dysregulation and autoimmunity in long COVID.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-8876163/v1).

- **Transfer and comparator:** Severe Long COVID donor communities → GF mice; comparison communities and BAFF-pathway intervention in mechanistic arms.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Barrier disruption, systemic immune/B-cell dysregulation, autoimmune features and neuroinflammation; BAFF neutralization improved selected abnormalities.
- **What this establishes:** A mechanistic transfer report, not evidence that all human Long COVID or treatment response is reproduced. March 2026 preprint; potential shared research resources with LC03. Antibody intervention is separate from the FMT itself.


### LC05 — Gulf War illness — immune components

**A Double-Humanized Mouse Model for Studying Host Gut Microbiome-Immune Interactions in Gulf War Illness.** 2024. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25116093); PMID 38892281.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Gulf War illness veteran fecal communities → mice with a humanized immune system; the paper also examines an independent chemical-exposure model.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Endotoxemia and inflammatory cytokine/receptor changes compatible with a microbiome–immune interaction.
- **What this establishes:** Preliminary component-level evidence in a specially humanized host. It does not establish transfer of the complete fatigue, pain and cognitive syndrome. The abstract does not resolve every donor-comparator detail.


### PA01 — Fibromyalgia pain

**The gut microbiota promotes pain in fibromyalgia.** 2025. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.03.032); PMID 40280127.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Fibromyalgia-patient versus healthy-control fecal communities → GF mice; replacing the community and immune/metabolic experiments provided additional tests.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Pain-related hypersensitivity with immune and metabolic changes; healthy-community replacement reduced pain.
- **What this establishes:** Direct support for a transferable contribution to fibromyalgia-related pain. A separate exploratory human treatment arm is rescue evidence, not another harmful-transfer experiment. The entire human syndrome was not reproduced.


### PA02 — Migraine-related hyperalgesia

**The gut microbiome modulates nitroglycerin-induced migraine-related hyperalgesia in mice.** 2021. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1177/03331024211050036); PMID 34644194.

- **Transfer and comparator:** One migraine patient versus one matched healthy donor → microbiota-conditioned mice subjected to a nitroglycerin migraine model.
- **What appeared in recipients:** More severe migraine-related hyperalgesia with patient microbiota.
- **What this establishes:** Challenge-dependent pain susceptibility, with exceptionally limited donor replication. It does not demonstrate spontaneous migraine or a universal migraine community.


## 2. Neurological, psychiatric, developmental and cognitive phenotypes


### N01 — Reduction of Abeta amyloid pathology in APPPS1 transgenic mice in the absence of gut microbiota.
Publication year: 2017. DOI: 10.1038/srep41802. PMID/record: 28176819. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28176819/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5297247/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Conventionally raised APPPS1 transgenic donors → germ-free APPPS1 recipients; WT-donor colonization and germ-free controls. Both donor and recipient are AD transgenic in the principal pathogenic-transfer arm.
- **Transferred outcome:** Greater cerebral amyloid-beta pathology after APPPS1 flora than WT flora.
- **Classification and caveat:** Amplification of genetically programmed amyloidosis; not induction of sporadic AD in normal mice. An erratum exists (PMID 28691712; DOI 10.1038/srep46856).

### N02 — Fecal metabolite of a gnotobiotic mouse transplanted with gut microbiota from a patient with Alzheimer's disease.
Publication year: 2019. DOI: 10.1080/09168451.2019.1644149. PMID/record: 31327302. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31327302/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** ONE AD patient vs ONE healthy volunteer → germ-free C57BL/6N mice; long-term humanized mice compared at 55 weeks.
- **Transferred outcome:** Worse object-location and object-recognition performance; altered fecal neuroactive metabolites.
- **Classification and caveat:** Single donor per condition makes disease-versus-donor attribution especially weak; cognitive phenotype, not demonstrated full AD.

### N03 — Transplantation of gut microbiota derived from Alzheimer's disease mouse model impairs memory function and neurogenesis in C57BL/6 mice.
Publication year: 2021. DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2021.09.002. PMID/record: 34500036. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34500036/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** 5xFAD mice → normal C57BL/6 mice; comparisons with WT-donor FMT and non-FMT controls. Recipient depletion status not established from accessible abstract.
- **Transferred outcome:** Memory impairment, reduced adult hippocampal neurogenesis and BDNF, elevated p21, microglial activation and colonic/systemic inflammation.
- **Classification and caveat:** Normal-genotype recipients establish transfer of cognitive/inflammatory features; amyloid/tau dementia was not demonstrated in this abstract.

### N04 — Transplantation of fecal microbiota from APP/PS1 mice and Alzheimer's disease patients enhanced endoplasmic reticulum stress in the cerebral cortex of wild-type mice.
Publication year: 2022. DOI: 10.3389/fnagi.2022.858130. PMID/record: 35966768. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35966768/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9367971/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Two arms: six AD human donors or APP/PS1 mouse donors → antibiotic-treated six-week male WT C57BL/6J mice. Controls were antibiotic-only and antibiotic-plus-PBS; no healthy-donor FMT control was found in the methods.
- **Transferred outcome:** Increased cortical endoplasmic-reticulum stress markers; attenuation with a TMAO-lowering intervention.
- **Classification and caveat:** Biochemical feature only, with NO healthy-donor FMT comparator: disease-specificity is not established. The authors explicitly did not test cognition, Aβ or p-tau in recipients. Dementia or memory loss was not demonstrated.

### N05 — Microbiota from Alzheimer's patients induce deficits in cognition and hippocampal neurogenesis.
Publication year: 2023. DOI: 10.1093/brain/awad303. PMID/record: 37849234. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37849234/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10689930/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** AD patients vs age-matched healthy donors → microbiota-depleted young adult rats.
- **Transferred outcome:** Impairment of hippocampal-neurogenesis-dependent memory functions and reduced adult hippocampal neurogenesis; recipient impairment correlated with donor cognitive scores.
- **Classification and caveat:** Cognition/neurogenesis transfer, not transfer of established Alzheimer neuropathology or a human AD diagnosis. Human-serum cell assay is a separate non-FMT experiment.

### N06 — Fecal microbiota transplantation derived from mild cognitive impairment individuals impairs cerebral glucose uptake and cognitive function in wild-type mice: *Bacteroidetes* and TXNIP-GLUT signaling pathway.
Publication year: 2024. DOI: 10.1080/19490976.2024.2395907. PMID/record: 39262376. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39262376/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11404573/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Early/late MCI donors → antibiotic-depleted wild-type mice; healthy-donor FMT and PBS vehicle control groups.
- **Transferred outcome:** Impaired learning/memory and cerebral FDG uptake; altered glucose transporters/TXNIP and brain/colon morphology.
- **Classification and caveat:** MCI is a syndrome with diverse causes, not equivalent to AD. 27-hydroxycholesterol and inhibitor experiments are separate mechanistic arms.

### N07 — Co-housing with Alzheimer's disease mice induces changes in gut microbiota and impairment of learning and memory in control mice.
Publication year: 2024. DOI: 10.1111/cns.14491. PMID/record: 37789692. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37789692/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11017403/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Female 3xTg-AD mice co-housed with age/sex-matched nontransgenic B6129SF2/J controls for eight months; separately housed groups as controls.
- **Transferred outcome:** Control cagemates developed learning/memory impairment, microbiome shifts, increased gut permeability and brain-associated changes.
- **Classification and caveat:** CO-HOUSING, not isolated FMT: shared social, environmental and microbial exposures prevent assigning all effects specifically to gut microbes.

### N08 — Gut microbiota transmission induces cognitive impairment through amyloid pathology in wild-type mice.
Publication year: 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuroscience.2026.02.041. PMID/record: 41759988. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41759988/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** One-month-old WT mice co-housed with age-matched 5XFAD mice for three months; WT housing comparison.
- **Transferred outcome:** WT cagemates developed impaired maze performance and elevated brain Aβ42 with AD-like microbial shifts.
- **Classification and caveat:** CO-HOUSING: early-life exposure and nonmicrobial cage effects; elevated Aβ42 is not equivalent to the complete AD syndrome.

### N09 — Gut Microbiota Regulate Motor Deficits and Neuroinflammation in a Model of Parkinson's Disease.
Publication year: 2016. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.018. PMID/record: 27912057. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27912057/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5718049/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** PD patients vs healthy donors → germ-free α-synuclein-overexpressing mice; WT genotype and germ-free/colonized comparisons.
- **Transferred outcome:** PD-donor communities enhanced motor impairments in αSyn-overexpressing recipients. Other arms show microbiota-dependent microglial activation and αSyn pathology.
- **Classification and caveat:** Human-donor result depended on αSyn genetic susceptibility; should not be described as PD spontaneously transmitted to ordinary WT mice.

### N10 — Neuroprotective effects of fecal microbiota transplantation on MPTP-induced Parkinson's disease mice: Gut microbiota, glial reaction and TLR4/TNF-α signaling pathway.
Publication year: 2018. DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2018.02.005. PMID/record: 29471030. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29471030/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** MPTP PD-model mouse microbiota → normal mice; also healthy FMT rescue of PD mice.
- **Transferred outcome:** Pathogenic-donor arm induced motor impairment and reduced striatal neurotransmitters in normal mice.
- **Classification and caveat:** Toxin-model-derived microbiota; distinguish pathogenic transfer from the better-known therapeutic rescue arm. Recipient depletion details not established in accessible abstract.

### N11 — Gut-first Parkinson's disease is encoded by gut dysbiome.
Publication year: 2024. DOI: 10.1186/s13024-024-00766-0. PMID/record: 39449004. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39449004/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11515425/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Five PD donors vs four age-matched healthy donors → conventional 32-week-old male WT C57BL/6 mice, with each recipient receiving one donor; untreated animals also included. No antibiotic depletion described in the recipient methods.
- **Transferred outcome:** Motor impairment, loss of midbrain TH-positive cells, gut/brain inflammation and αSyn-associated pathology.
- **Classification and caveat:** Initial phenotypes used multiple donors, but much mechanistic work used one PD/one control donor; PD donors were selected for constipation. Stronger WT induction evidence than Sampson but still a mouse phenotype.

### N12 — Fecal microbiota from patients with Parkinson's disease intensifies inflammation and neurodegeneration in A53T mice.
Publication year: 2024. DOI: 10.1111/cns.70003. PMID/record: 39161161. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39161161/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11333719/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Four PD vs four healthy donors → conventional presymptomatic seven-month A53T αSyn transgenic and WT mice, four donor/genotype groups. No antibiotic pretreatment described in the methods examined.
- **Transferred outcome:** PD microbiota worsened inflammation, barrier damage, glial activation, αSyn deposition and dopaminergic cell loss in A53T recipients.
- **Classification and caveat:** Genotype-dependent aggravation. Equivalent pathology was not demonstrated in WT recipients; the paper emphasizes A53T effects.

### N13 — Effects of Gut Microbiota Alterations on Motor, Gastrointestinal, and Behavioral Phenotype in a Mouse Model of Parkinson's Disease.
Publication year: 2022. DOI: 10.3233/jpd-223165. PMID/record: 35599496. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35599496/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** SNCA A53T transgenic mice: microbiota disrupted by antibiotics or exchanged through co-housing with WT animals.
- **Transferred outcome:** Despite major microbial alterations, motor, GI and behavioral phenotypes changed minimally.
- **Classification and caveat:** Negative modification experiment, not negative human-PD-donor FMT replication. Supports model dependence rather than refuting all PD transfers.

### N14 — Human Gut Microbiota from Autism Spectrum Disorder Promote Behavioral Symptoms in Mice.
Publication year: 2019. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.004. PMID/record: 31150625. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31150625/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993574/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Human ASD vs typically developing donors → germ-free WT C57BL/6J parents colonized at weaning, bred, with adult OFFSPRING behavior tested.
- **Transferred outcome:** Offspring of ASD-colonized parents showed increased repetitive behavior, reduced vocalization and reduced direct social interaction, plus brain splicing changes.
- **Classification and caveat:** Developmental/vertical-exposure design, not simply adult FMT. Mild-ASD donors lacked main deficits; three-chamber sociability was not different. Effects differed by donor/sex.

### N15 — Fecal Microbiome Transplantation from Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder Modulates Tryptophan and Serotonergic Synapse Metabolism and Induces Altered Behaviors in Germ-Free Mice.
Publication year: 2021. DOI: 10.1128/msystems.01343-20. PMID/record: 33824200. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33824200/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8547010/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Pooled feces from five ASD boys vs five typically developing boys → germ-free mice; behavioral testing after colonization.
- **Transferred outcome:** ASD-associated behavior (grooming/social/olfactory readouts), altered tryptophan/serotonin metabolism and related colonic/prefrontal proteins.
- **Classification and caveat:** Pooling removes donor-level replication; mice reproduce selected behaviors, not the clinical diagnosis. Full text also compares germ-free baseline.

### N16 — Mouse strain-specific responses along the gut-brain axis upon fecal microbiota transplantation from children with autism.
Publication year: 2025. DOI: 10.1080/19490976.2024.2447822. PMID/record: 39773319. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39773319/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11730631/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Children with ASD and GI symptoms vs their neurotypical siblings → juvenile conventional male BALB/c and C57BL/6J mice depleted by PEG bowel cleansing (not antibiotics); conventional water controls.
- **Transferred outcome:** BALB/c recipients showed reduced exploration/social behavior and inflammatory/metabolic changes; C57BL/6J recipients showed fewer changes.
- **Classification and caveat:** Critical partial/strain-dependent corroboration; small pooled-donor groups and six recipients/group; absence of post-PEG baseline microbiota measurement.

### N17 — Gut microbiota transfer from the preclinical maternal immune activation model of autism is sufficient to induce sex-specific alterations in immune response and behavioural outcomes.
Publication year: 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2024.10.030. PMID/record: 39471905. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39471905/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Male/female maternal-immune-activation (MIA) model donors vs vehicle donors → otherwise unmanipulated four-week C57BL/6 recipients of both sexes.
- **Transferred outcome:** Male MIA microbiota reduced sociability and increased neuroinflammation, strongest in male recipients; donor microbiota of either sex altered vocalizations/anxiety.
- **Classification and caveat:** MIA is an induced developmental model, not diagnosed autism. Sex of both donors and recipients strongly determined outcome.

### N18 — The gut microbiome from patients with schizophrenia modulates the glutamate-glutamine-GABA cycle and schizophrenia-relevant behaviors in mice.
Publication year: 2019. DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aau8317. PMID/record: 30775438. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30775438/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6365110/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** SCZ patients vs healthy donors → germ-free mice.
- **Transferred outcome:** Hyperactivity, exaggerated acoustic startle, reduced hippocampal glutamate and increased glutamine/GABA.
- **Classification and caveat:** No significant PPI, Y-maze, sociability or social-novelty deficits. Psychosis and the full schizophrenia syndrome were not shown to transfer. Correction DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aay2759.

### N19 — Transplantation of microbiota from drug-free patients with schizophrenia causes schizophrenia-like abnormal behaviors and dysregulated kynurenine metabolism in mice.
Publication year: 2020. DOI: 10.1038/s41380-019-0475-4. PMID/record: 31391545. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31391545/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Drug-free SCZ patients vs healthy donors → antibiotic-treated SPF mice.
- **Transferred outcome:** Psychomotor hyperactivity and impaired learning/memory; increased peripheral/brain kynurenine–kynurenic-acid pathway and altered monoamines.
- **Classification and caveat:** 2019 online / 2020 journal year; nonspecific behavioral endophenotypes, not a diagnosis of schizophrenia.

### N20 — Transplantation of gut microbiota derived from patients with schizophrenia induces schizophrenia-like behaviors and dysregulated brain transcript response in mice.
Publication year: 2024. DOI: 10.1038/s41537-024-00460-6. PMID/record: 38589422. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38589422/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11001608/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Five SCZ patients (all receiving antipsychotics) vs five healthy donors → antibiotic-treated male SPF C57BL/6 mice.
- **Transferred outcome:** Hyperactivity, sociability deficits, dysregulated brain transcription and alternative splicing.
- **Classification and caveat:** Independent behavioral corroboration, but donor antipsychotic treatment is a confound; no proof that all SCZ pathology or human psychosis is transmitted.

### N21 — Gut microbiome remodeling induces depressive-like behaviors through a pathway mediated by the host's metabolism.
Publication year: 2016. DOI: 10.1038/mp.2016.44. PMID/record: 27067014. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27067014/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** MDD patients vs healthy donors → germ-free mice; conventionally raised and germ-free comparisons in other arms.
- **Transferred outcome:** Depression-like behavioral changes with altered carbohydrate/amino-acid microbial and host metabolism.
- **Classification and caveat:** Germ-free animals have atypical baseline behavior/development; behavior assays do not establish the full human syndrome.

### N22 — Transferring the blues: Depression-associated gut microbiota induces neurobehavioural changes in the rat.
Publication year: 2016. DOI: 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2016.07.019. PMID/record: 27491067. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27491067/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** MDD patients vs matched healthy controls → microbiota-depleted rats; donors selected from 34 cases/33 controls.
- **Transferred outcome:** Anhedonia and anxiety-like behavior with altered tryptophan metabolism.
- **Classification and caveat:** Rat behavioral features and biochemical pathways, not transfer of a clinical MDD diagnosis; actual FMT subgroup smaller than recruited clinical cohort.

### N23 — Effect of gut microbiota on depressive-like behaviors in mice is mediated by the endocannabinoid system.
Publication year: 2020. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-19931-2. PMID/record: 33311466. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33311466/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7732982/)

- **Donor → recipient / controls:** Unpredictable-chronic-mild-stress mouse microbiota → adult germ-free C57BL/6J mice, with replication in antibiotic-treated naive adult male recipients and subsequent mechanism experiments in antibiotic-treated mice; unstressed-donor controls.
- **Transferred outcome:** Transferred depression-associated behavioral/cellular phenotypes, reduced central endocannabinoid signaling and hippocampal neurogenesis.
- **Classification and caveat:** Stress-model donors; mechanistic rescue supports eCB involvement but cannot show human MDD transmission.

### I01 — MS: spontaneous EAE in predisposed transgenic mice
Berer K et al. **Gut microbiota from multiple sclerosis patients enables spontaneous autoimmune encephalomyelitis in mice.** PNAS, 2017. DOI **10.1073/pnas.1711233114**; PMID **28893994**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28893994/) ; [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711233114) .

**Transfer:** H→A, fecal microbiota from monozygotic twins discordant for MS into germ-free, myelin-reactive TCR-transgenic RR SJL/J mice (6 weeks old in figure caption). Healthy cotwin stool is the principal control; GF mice provide baseline. The donor cohort is larger than the subset used for colonization. **Result:** MS-derived communities produced a significantly higher incidence of spontaneous autoimmune encephalomyelitis than healthy-twin communities; recipient immune cells showed less IL-10. **Causal scope:** disease facilitation in a strongly genetically primed host without active EAE immunization; not induction of MS in normal mice or humans. ### Whole-community experiments

1. **Turnbaugh et al., 2006 — An obesity-associated gut microbiome with increased capacity for energy harvest.** *Nature* 444:1027–1031. DOI **10.1038/nature05414**; PMID **17183312**. ### Soiled-bedding transfer (not gavage FMT; keep separate)

## 5. Cardiovascular and renal disease


### Hypertension

1. **Li J, et al. (2017). Gut microbiota dysbiosis contributes to the development of hypertension. Microbiome 5:14.** DOI: **10.1186/s40168-016-0222-x**; PMID **28143587**. [Primary full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40168-016-0222-x); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28143587/).

   - **Controls/caveat:** healthy-donor FMT and engraftment profiling; only three human donors, tail-cuff measurement at a single post-transfer time point, isolator effects possible. Discussion says the same result was not obtained in conventionally raised mice. Strong landmark for a donor-associated BP phenotype; limited generalizability.

2. **Adnan S, et al. (2017; online 2016). Alterations in the gut microbiota can elicit hypertension in rats. Physiological Genomics 49:96–104.** DOI: **10.1152/physiolgenomics.00081.2016**; PMID **28011881**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28011881/); [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5336599/).
   - **Donor → recipient:** pooled cecal contents from stroke-prone spontaneously hypertensive rats (**SHRSP**, not ordinary SHR donors) or normotensive WKY rats → antibiotic-pretreated, four-week-old WKY or SHR recipients by gavage; four donor–recipient combinations.

   - **Controls/caveat:** reciprocal strain transfers, weekly tail cuff and 16S profiling. WKY→SHR reduced BP only nonsignificantly. Small groups (6–7), pooled donors, genetic background and antibiotic conditioning restrict inference. Elevated BP transfer is better wording than transmission of the entire SHR phenotype.

3. **Durgan DJ, et al. (2016; online 2015). Role of the Gut Microbiome in Obstructive Sleep Apnea-Induced Hypertension. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4713369/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26711739/).

   - **Controls/caveat:** importantly, the same contents **did not raise BP in naïve recipients without OSA**. Chow-fed OSA normally did not raise BP. Thus the transferred community increased susceptibility to an OSA co-trigger; it was insufficient by itself. Small recipient groups (n=4). Exact antibiotic conditioning of the transfer arm is in supplement and was not independently recovered here; do not infer it from the separate antibiotic experiment.

### Atherosclerosis / thrombosis

4. **Gregory JC, et al. (2015; online 2014). Transmission of atherosclerosis susceptibility with gut microbial transplantation. Journal of Biological Chemistry 290:5647–5660.** DOI: **10.1074/jbc.M114.618249**; PMID **25550161**. TMA/TMAO separation was initially evident but did not persist to the endpoint, so the plaque result should not be compressed into a claim of stable TMAO mediation. This is actual lesion-severity transfer, not merely a biomarker association.

   - **Controls/caveat:** low-TMAO donors, chow arms, germ-free/no-transfer controls; separate direct-TMAO and conventionalization experiments. This is **thrombosis potential after experimental injury**, not spontaneous heart attacks/strokes. The full-community Figure 5 arm qualifies independently of the paper's metabolite-only arms; environmental conventionalization should be labeled separately.

### Important negative / counterintuitive transfer context

11. **Mell B, et al. (2015). Evidence for a link between gut microbiota and hypertension in the Dahl rat. Physiological Genomics 47:187–197.** DOI: **10.1152/physiolgenomics.00136.2014**; PMID **25829393**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25829393/); [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4451389/).
   - **Donor → recipient:** cecal contents exchanged between Dahl salt-sensitive (S) and salt-resistant (R) rats after antibiotics, with high-salt feeding.
   - **Result:** S-donor microbiota did **not** raise BP in resistant recipients. Counterintuitively, **R-donor** microbiota raised BP and shortened lifespan in S recipients relative to S→S controls.
   - **Interpretation and control:** reciprocal strain transfers show strong host-genotype dependence; this must not be cited as conventional “hypertensive donor → healthy recipient” transmission. It is whole-community transfer of a harmful effect in a susceptible recipient, despite a resistant donor phenotype. Particularly valuable against overgeneralized claims that donor disease labels predict recipient outcome.

### Direct fecal/cecal transfers and gut-community transplants


#### IBS visceral hypersensitivity

**Constante M et al. (2022)**. Biogeographic Variation and Functional Pathways of the Gut Microbiota in Celiac Disease. DOI: [10.1053/j.gastro.2022.06.088](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2022.06.088); PMID: [35810781](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35810781/).

- **Transfer and comparator:** Human duodenal microbiota from active celiac vs genotype-stratified controls → GF mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Celiac-donor microbiota impaired gluten degradation.
- **What this establishes:** Duodenal rather than fecal transfer; metabolic phenotype only. Does not demonstrate full celiac autoimmunity/villous atrophy.


#### Preclinical UC inflammatory/proteolytic phenotype

**Galipeau HJ et al. (2021)**. Novel Fecal Biomarkers That Precede Clinical Diagnosis of Ulcerative Colitis. DOI: [10.1053/j.gastro.2020.12.004](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2020.12.004); PMID: [33310084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33310084/).

- **Transfer and comparator:** Pre-UC samples from initially healthy people later developing UC, post-UC and matched controls → GF adults or dams/offspring.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Pre-UC communities transmitted higher proteolytic activity and inflammatory immune tone.
- **What this establishes:** Predisease donor and subclinical recipient endpoints. Useful temporal evidence, not spontaneous clinical colitis transmission.


#### IBS-D-like physiology, single species

**Zhai L et al. (2023)**. Ruminococcus gnavus plays a pathogenic role in diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome by increasing serotonin biosynthesis. DOI: [10.1016/j.chom.2022.11.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chom.2022.11.006); PMID: [36495868](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36495868/).

- **Transfer and comparator:** Ruminococcus gnavus monocolonization → GF mice; mechanistic metabolite/TAAR1 interventions.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Increased GI transit and colonic secretion via phenethylamine/tryptamine-dependent enterochromaffin serotonin production; TAAR1 inhibition blocked effects.
- **What this establishes:** Defined organism, not whole FMT or a disease-donor community. Print 2023; online December 2022.


#### FAP/CRC tumorigenic defined pair

**Dejea CM et al. (2018)**. Patients with familial adenomatous polyposis harbor colonic biofilms containing tumorigenic bacteria. DOI: [10.1126/science.aah3648](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah3648); PMID: [29420293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29420293/). Full text: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5881113/)

- **Transfer and comparator:** Colibactin-positive E. coli plus enterotoxigenic B. fragilis → tumor-prone mice, compared with either species alone.
- **What appeared in recipients:** More IL-17/DNA damage, earlier tumors and mortality.
- **What this establishes:** Cultured two-species mechanism, not transplantation of complete FAP stool or mucosal biofilm. Requires tumor-prone host.


## 7. Pregnancy, reproductive conditions and thyroid disease


### RP01 — Preeclampsia

**Gut dysbiosis induces the development of pre-eclampsia through bacterial translocation.** 2020. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2019-319101); PMID 31900289.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Preeclampsia-patient versus normotensive-pregnancy feces → antibiotic-conditioned mice assessed before and during pregnancy.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Higher blood pressure, pregnancy-associated proteinuria, fetal/placental abnormalities and intestinal/immune disruption.
- **What this establishes:** Human-community transfer of preeclampsia-like features in pregnancy. It does not imply preeclampsia occurs without pregnancy or that a single taxon explains all cases.


### RP02 — Preeclampsia severity

**Gut Dysbiosis Promotes Preeclampsia by Regulating Macrophages and Trophoblasts.** 2022. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.122.320771); PMID 35950704.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Preeclampsia versus normotensive-pregnancy donor communities → rats with an experimental preeclampsia model.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Patient-community transfer worsened disease features; healthy-community transfer was protective, with macrophage/trophoblast changes.
- **What this establishes:** Conditional worsening of an existing model, not unconditioned induction. Separate Akkermansia and short-chain-fatty-acid experiments support candidate mechanisms.


### RP03 — Gestational-diabetes-associated hyperglycemia

**Perturbations of gut microbiota in gestational diabetes mellitus patients induce hyperglycemia in germ-free mice.** 2020. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S2040174420000768); PMID 32924908.

- **Transfer and comparator:** GDM-patient versus matched healthy-pregnancy feces → GF mice; the human profiling cohort had 45 participants per group.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Hyperglycemia after patient-community transfer.
- **What this establishes:** Functional evidence for a glucose-regulating effect. The profiling cohort size is not the number of independently transplanted donors; pregnancy of recipients was not established from the retrieved abstract.


### RP04 — GDM features and offspring effects

**Gut microbiota in women with gestational diabetes mellitus has potential impact on metabolism in pregnant mice and their offspring.** 2022. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2022.870422); PMID 35992705.

- **Transfer and comparator:** GDM versus healthy-pregnancy donor communities → GF pregnant mice; offspring were also followed.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Maternal hyperglycemia, inflammatory changes and hepatic fat; offspring weight/glucose and community differences.
- **What this establishes:** Maternal metabolic and developmental effects in a pregnancy context; offspring changes are not a separate direct FMT experiment.


### RP05 — Prediagnostic GDM-associated community

**Gestational diabetes is driven by microbiota-induced inflammation months before diagnosis.** 2023. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2022-328406); PMID 36627187.

- **Transfer and comparator:** First-trimester feces from women who later developed GDM versus controls → GF female mice; transfer work used three cohorts.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Impaired glucose tolerance and inflammatory changes before the human donors had received a GDM diagnosis.
- **What this establishes:** Especially useful temporal evidence: the donor-associated state preceded diagnosis. Mouse pregnancy is not established in the main evidence examined, so the recipient endpoint is glucose impairment rather than automatically GDM.


### RP06 — GDM inflammation and insulin resistance

**Fecal microbiota transplantation from gestational diabetes mellitus patients induces glucose intolerance and subclinical inflammation in mice.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1723816); PMID 41684898.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Mid/late-pregnancy GDM versus healthy donor communities → antibiotic-conditioned pregnant mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Glucose intolerance, insulin resistance, gestational weight and subclinical inflammatory changes.
- **What this establishes:** Pregnancy-context metabolic transfer. Other diet/drug diabetes-model arms are separate. The article has a 2025 DOI and 2026 indexed publication.


### RP07 — GDM bile acids and resistin

**Gut microbiota contributes to gestational diabetes mellitus by interfering with bile acid metabolism and resistin.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2026.1675560); PMID 41778019.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Trimester-specific GDM versus control communities → antibiotic-conditioned pregnant mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Glucose intolerance, impaired barrier function and bile-acid/LPS/resistin changes.
- **What this establishes:** Mechanistic extension of pregnancy-associated dysregulation. Shared investigators/institution with RP06 mean it should not automatically be counted as a wholly independent cohort replication.


### RP08 — Endometriosis lesion progression

**Antibiotic therapy with metronidazole reduces endometriosis disease progression in mice: a potential role for gut microbiota.** 2019. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dez041); PMID 31037294.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Endometriosis-model mouse fecal material restored to antibiotic-treated recipients bearing experimentally established lesions; control-community comparisons.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Restoration/promotion of lesion growth and inflammatory features after microbiota depletion.
- **What this establishes:** Community-dependent progression of an implanted/surgically established disease model. Feces did not by themselves create ectopic endometrial tissue.


### RP09 — Endometriosis microbial/metabolic contribution

**Gut microbiota and microbiota-derived metabolites promotes endometriosis.** 2023. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41420-023-01309-0); PMID 36693853.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Endometriosis-model versus control mouse communities → microbiota-depleted recipients with established endometriosis lesions.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Greater lesion growth; microbial-metabolite experiments supplied additional mechanistic evidence.
- **What this establishes:** Conditional disease progression, not de novo endometriosis. The quinic-acid experiment is not itself FMT.


### RP10 — Human endometriosis community and NET-associated progression

**Intraperitoneal translocation of gut microbiota induces NETosis and promotes endometriosis.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2025-336185); PMID 41320323.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Human endometriosis donor communities → mice with an endometriosis model; bacterial-tracking and mechanistic arms also examined.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Barrier dysfunction, gut-to-peritoneal bacterial translocation, neutrophil extracellular traps and greater lesion progression.
- **What this establishes:** A human-community extension of conditional progression. Separate Pseudomonas experiments should not be generalized to all strains or called whole FMT.


### TH01 — Graves disease susceptibility

**Gut Dysbiosis Contributes to the Imbalance of Treg and Th17 Cells in Graves' Disease Patients by Propionic Acid.** 2020. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgaa511); PMID 32785703.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Graves-patient versus healthy microbiota → mice in an experimental Graves model.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Higher disease incidence and Th17/Treg-related abnormalities; microbial propionate mechanisms examined separately.
- **What this establishes:** Disease-model susceptibility. The verified evidence does not establish spontaneous Graves disease in unprimed recipients.


### TH02 — Graves orbitopathy-related disease severity

**Modulating gut microbiota in a mouse model of Graves' orbitopathy and its impact on induced disease.** 2021. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-020-00952-4); PMID 33593429.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Severe Graves-orbitopathy human microbiota → mice receiving a TSH-receptor immune challenge; water/control-treatment comparisons.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Worse hyperthyroidism/autoantibody and orbital adipose-related outcomes.
- **What this establishes:** Immune-challenge-dependent Graves/orbitopathy features. Lack of a fully matched healthy-human-FMT comparator limits disease-specific attribution.


### TH03 — Primary hypothyroidism: hormone component

**Gut dysbiosis is associated with primary hypothyroidism with interaction on gut-thyroid axis.** 2020. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1042/CS20200475); PMID 32519746.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Primary-hypothyroidism versus healthy human donor communities → mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Lower circulating total thyroxine after hypothyroid-donor transfer.
- **What this establishes:** A thyroid-hormone phenotype; this is not sufficient to claim transfer of autoimmune Hashimoto thyroiditis. Recipient conditioning was not resolved from the accessible abstract.


### TH04 — Hashimoto-associated thyroiditis severity

**Gut Microecology May Be Involved in the Pathogenesis of Hashimoto Thyroiditis by Reducing Production of Hydrogen Sulfide.** 2024; online 2023. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgad588); PMID 37793163.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Hashimoto-patient versus healthy donor FMT → female mice with experimental autoimmune thyroiditis; additional hydrogen-sulfide intervention.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Greater thyroiditis and Th1/Th17 abnormalities with patient FMT; lower microbial sulfide-production capacity and circulating sulfide.
- **What this establishes:** Exacerbation of induced autoimmune thyroiditis. It does not show Hashimoto disease arising from FMT alone. The sulfide rescue is a model-specific mechanistic result, not a general instruction to increase gut sulfide.


### TH05 — Graves-orbitopathy donor inflammatory components

**Modulation of gut microbiota in Graves' orbitopathy: Prevotella dominance and atorvastatin's impact.** 2025. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-025-02219-2); PMID 41462485.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Graves-orbitopathy-associated human material → antibiotic-conditioned mice in a study comparing GO, Graves disease and healthy communities.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Slower weight gain, barrier injury and systemic inflammatory/LPS-binding-protein changes.
- **What this establishes:** The verified abstract supports systemic/intestinal components; orbital disease itself was not demonstrated, unlike the endpoint studied in TH02.


## 8. Lung, bone, muscle, kidney-stone and pancreatic complications


### LU01 — COPD-related lung inflammation

**Gut microbiota dysbiosis contributes to the development of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.** 2021. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-021-01872-z); PMID 34696775.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Human COPD versus healthy donor communities → mice; separate extended biomass-smoke plus FMT arm.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Patient-community transfer induced lung inflammatory features. With smoke exposure, recipients developed more emphysema, mucus/airway remodeling and impaired lung function.
- **What this establishes:** FMT-alone inflammation must be distinguished from fuller COPD-like pathology requiring smoke. The human profiling cohort is not the independent transfer-donor count.


### BO01 — Osteoporosis-related bone loss: human donors

**Fecal microbiota transplantation from postmenopausal osteoporosis human donors accelerated bone mass loss in mice.** 2024. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2024.1488017); PMID 39703374.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Five postmenopausal osteoporosis versus five nonosteoporotic postmenopausal women → antibiotic-conditioned mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Accelerated bone-mass loss, impaired gut barrier and inflammatory changes.
- **What this establishes:** Human-community transfer of a bone-loss phenotype. Small donor and recipient samples limit generalizability; it does not identify a universal osteoporosis-causing organism.


### BO02 — Ovariectomy-associated bone-loss community

**Fecal microbiota transplantation reveals the impact of gut microbiota dysbiosis on osteoporosis development in ovariectomized mice.** 2025. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/femsle/fnaf081); PMID 40796104.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Ovariectomized versus control mouse communities → healthy recipients; reverse healthy-to-ovariectomized transfer also studied.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Bone-loss features after disease-model community transfer; the reverse transfer did not clearly restore short-term trabecular structure.
- **What this establishes:** Animal-model transfer, with asymmetry between harmful induction and therapeutic rescue. Not evidence that FMT reliably reverses established osteoporosis.


### BO03 — Ovariectomy community: negative bone transfer

**Ovariectomy-Induced Dysbiosis May Have a Minor Effect on Bone in Mice.** 2021. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9122563); PMID 34946163.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Ovariectomized versus sham cecal communities → antibiotic-conditioned intact or ovariectomized mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** No meaningful between-community differences in bone mineral density or the measured bone-turnover markers.
- **What this establishes:** An explicit negative under this recipient and follow-up context; it prevents treating bone loss as uniformly transferable.


### BO04 — Osteoarthritis progression: metabolic donors

**Faecal microbiota transplantation from metabolically compromised human donors accelerates osteoarthritis in mice.** 2020. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2019-216471); PMID 32205337.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Human OA with metabolic syndrome, OA without metabolic syndrome, or healthy communities → GF mice with a meniscal/ligament injury model.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Metabolically compromised OA-donor material accelerated joint degeneration, synovitis and inflammatory/barrier changes.
- **What this establishes:** Injury-conditioned OA progression, not spontaneous arthritis caused by feces. Donor pooling reduces individual-donor inference.


### BO05 — Osteoarthritis progression without donor obesity

**Gut microbiota from osteoarthritic patients without obesity aggravates osteoarthritis progression in rats by enriching acetic acid.** 2025. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micpath.2025.107911); PMID 40683544.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Nonobese OA, obese OA, obese non-OA and healthy donor communities → antibiotic-conditioned rats with ligament-injury OA.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Nonobese OA-donor microbiota produced marked cartilage injury, synovitis and acetate-associated inflammatory changes.
- **What this establishes:** Conditional progression. This does not support a universal rule that the heaviest donor has the most harmful OA community; a correlated taxon is not established as the sole cause.


### MU01 — Hemodialysis-associated sarcopenia

**The gut microbiota from maintenance hemodialysis patients with sarcopenia influences muscle function in mice.** 2023. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2023.1225991); PMID 37771694.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Ten maintenance-hemodialysis patients with versus ten without sarcopenia → antibiotic-conditioned mice.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Reduced muscle mass/function with sarcopenic-donor communities.
- **What this establishes:** A muscle phenotype with a useful renal-disease-matched comparator. It is not evidence for every cause of sarcopenia.


### MU02 — Heat-exposure-associated muscle impairment

**Heat exposure promotes sarcopenia via gut microbiota-derived metabolites.** 2024. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.14370); PMID 39468887.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Heat-exposed versus control mouse microbiota → GF recipients.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Muscle dysfunction/atrophy-related changes; homocitrulline and ferroptosis were investigated as mediators.
- **What this establishes:** Transfer of an environmentally induced adverse muscle state, not a patient-derived diagnosis. Metabolite arms are separate from FMT.


### KI01 — Calcium-oxalate stone risk: biochemical features

**Unraveling the role of gut microbiota by fecal microbiota transplantation in rat model of kidney stone disease.** 2024. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-72694-4); PMID 39300177.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Eight calcium-oxalate-stone patients versus ten healthy donors → antibiotic-conditioned rats.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Higher urinary oxalate/pH and supersaturation-related changes; renal pathology did not demonstrate corresponding stone disease.
- **What this establishes:** Transfer of stone-risk chemistry, not proven new stones. This distinction is important when interpreting a donor profile as a disease-transmission risk.


### KI02 — High-salt community and stone-model injury

**High-Salt Diet Promotes Kidney Stone Formation Through Gut Microbiota-Dependent Inflammatory Pathways.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202503466r); PMID 42054188.

- **Transfer and comparator:** High-salt-diet versus comparison mouse communities → recipients in a kidney-stone experimental setting.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Higher TMAO, renal inflammatory changes and calcium-oxalate deposition.
- **What this establishes:** A diet-conditioned microbial contribution to stone-model injury. The accessible abstract does not fully resolve additional stone-inducing conditioning; no claim of spontaneous stones in otherwise unchallenged mice is warranted.


### PN01 — Acute pancreatitis severity

**Gut microbiota dysbiosis exacerbates acute pancreatitis via Escherichia coli-driven neutrophil heterogeneity and NETosis.** 2025. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2025.2606480); PMID 41439481.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Acute-pancreatitis-associated human microbiota → mice with experimental pancreatitis; separate E. coli mechanistic arm.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Greater pancreatic injury and neutrophil/NET-associated inflammation.
- **What this establishes:** Worsening of an induced pancreatitis model, not pancreatitis arising from stool alone. Findings for an investigated E. coli isolate do not generalize to all E. coli.


### PN02 — Chronic-pancreatitis-associated anxiety/depression

**Dysregulation of the microbiota-gut-brain axis induced by chronic pancreatitis mediates anxiety- and depression-like behaviors in mice.** 2026. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1753424); PMID 41613143.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Chronic-pancreatitis-model versus control mouse microbiota → recipient mice in antibiotic/FMT experiments.
- **What appeared in recipients:** CP-derived communities independently induced anxiety/depression-like behaviors with barrier, inflammatory and brain-signaling abnormalities.
- **What this establishes:** Transfer of psychological/physiological complications, not proof that chronic pancreatitis itself transferred. Separate probiotic improvement is rescue evidence.


### SL01 — Insomnia-like sleep disturbance

**Gut microbiota regulate insomnia-like behaviors via gut-brain metabolic axis.** 2025; online 2024. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-024-02867-0); PMID 39658705.

- **Transfer and comparator:** Insomnia-patient versus comparison human donor communities → GF mice; separate microbial-metabolite rescue experiments.
- **What appeared in recipients:** Insomnia-like sleep behavior and lower circulating butyrate; butyrate intervention reversed selected sleep abnormalities.
- **What this establishes:** Direct human-community transfer of a sleep phenotype. It does not establish that every insomnia case has this mechanism or that the rescue is a proven clinical treatment.


### Retinal disease: relevant transfer studies that do not establish disease-donor induction

Two frequently relevant papers mainly demonstrate protection by healthier communities. In [Andriessen et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.15252/emmm.201606531), regular-diet microbiota reduced laser-induced choroidal neovascularization in high-fat-fed mice. The design did not include high-fat-donor FMT into regular-diet recipients; differences between those diet groups cannot be assigned to donor microbiota alone. In [the 2022 obesity-related retinopathy/nephropathy paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2022.1040846), healthier db/m-donor FMT improved already diabetic db/db recipients. These support microbiome modulation of retinal injury, but should not be counted as confirmed transfer of human AMD or diabetic retinopathy from diseased donors.

## 10. Alcohol-related liver injury, encephalopathy, biliary disease and gout


1. **Llopis M et al. 2016 (online2015). Intestinal microbiota contributes to individual susceptibility to alcoholic liver disease.** *Gut*65:830–839. **DOI10.1136/gutjnl-2015-310585; PMID26642859.** [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26642859/). **Transfer:** microbiota from patients with severe alcoholic hepatitis versus alcohol-consuming patients without hepatitis → humanized GF and conventional mouse models receiving alcohol. **Result:** severe-hepatitis donor microbiota produced greater liver inflammation/necrosis, gut permeability and bacterial translocation. **Control/caveat:** alcohol-exposed no-hepatitis donor comparator; phenotype is alcohol-conditioned susceptibility/severity, not alcohol-independent hepatitis transmission. A later no-hepatitis-community transfer improved lesions in conventional mice; the harmful donor comparison qualifies independently of that rescue.

2. **Duan Y et al. 2019. Bacteriophage targeting of gut bacterium attenuates alcoholic liver disease.** *Nature*575:505–511. **DOI10.1038/s41586-019-1742-x; PMID31723265; PMCIDPMC6872939.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6872939/). **Transfer:** whole fecal communities from two cytolysin-positive versus two cytolysin-negative alcoholic-hepatitis patients → GF C57BL/6 mice in an ethanol-exposure model. **Result:** cytolysin-positive-donor communities caused greater ethanol-related liver injury, steatosis and inflammatory/fibrogenic expression. **Controls/caveat:** cytolysin-negative disease-donor comparison and non-alcohol control diets; narrow donor/strain context and alcohol co-trigger. **Separate mechanisms:** isogenic cytolytic/non-cytolytic E.faecalis and targeted phage experiments support the mechanism; these are not additional whole-FMT studies.

3. **Liu R et al. 2020 (online2019; Bajaj senior author). Neuroinflammation in Murine Cirrhosis Is Dependent on the Gut Microbiome and Is Attenuated by Fecal Transplant.** *Hepatology*71:611–626. **DOI10.1002/hep.30827; PMID31220352; PMCIDPMC6923631.** [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6923631/). **Transfer:** stool from cirrhosis patients with minimal hepatic encephalopathy versus healthy subjects → GF C57BL/6 mice; another experiment compared stool from recurrent-HE patients before versus after therapeutic FMT. **Result:** cirrhosis-donor recipients had higher cortical neuroinflammation, microglial/astroglial activation and altered GABA/synaptic-plasticity expression. **Crucial distinction:** donor stool did **not** cause liver histological injury; whole-FMT neuroinflammatory effects were observed independently of active liver injury in recipients. Separate GF/conventional CCl4-cirrhosis arms supplied disease-context comparators. **Controls/caveat:** healthy donor, pre/post-treatment donor and sterile-supernatant controls; sterile preparations did not reproduce the same brain effects, but this does not exclude continuously produced metabolites. Brain molecular endpoints are not the complete clinical HE syndrome.

4. **He X et al. 2025. The gut-brain axis underlying hepatic encephalopathy in liver cirrhosis.** *Nat Med*31:627–638. **DOI10.1038/s41591-024-03405-9; PMID39779925.** [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03405-9); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39779925/). **Transfer:** stool from three HE patients versus three healthy donors → GF **cirrhotic** mice (multiple recipients per donor). **Result:** donor-community transfer reproduced neurobehavioral/neuropathological HE-like changes, including memory impairment and cortical injury, with brain phenylethylamine accumulation. **Controls/caveat:** healthy-donor and GF saline controls; pre-existing chemically induced cirrhosis is essential recipient context, so this is transfer of encephalopathy susceptibility/severity, not induction of cirrhosis. **Separate mechanisms:** Ruminococcus gnavus colonization produced HE-like manifestations in cirrhotic but not healthy mice; targeting bacterial phenylalanine decarboxylase or phenylethylamine reversed effects.

5. **Nakamoto N et al. 2019. Gut pathobionts underlie intestinal barrier dysfunction and liver T helper 17 cell immune response in primary sclerosing cholangitis.** *Nat Microbiol*4:492–503. **DOI10.1038/s41564-018-0333-1; PMID30643240.** [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-018-0333-1); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30643240/). **Transfer:** PSC/ulcerative-colitis patient fecal communities → gnotobiotic mice, with a DDC-induced hepatobiliary-injury challenge in disease-severity experiments. **Result:** liver TH17 priming, barrier dysfunction/bacterial translocation and greater susceptibility to hepatobiliary injury. **Controls/caveat:** Fig.1 verifies four healthy donors, five PSC/UC donors and five UC-only donors, plus GF controls. Fig.2 liver-injury challenge compares healthy-donor recipients, PSC/UC-donor recipients and GF mice. Do not call this spontaneous full PSC transmission. **Separate mechanisms:** K.pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis and Enterococcus gallinarum were recovered from recipient mesenteric nodes; defined-bacterial and organoid work identified K.pneumoniae-associated epithelial pore formation. Those isolate/consortium experiments must remain separate from the genuine whole-human-community transfer arm.

### Gout and hyperuricemia

No verified **gout/hyperuricemia patient stool → animal harmful-transfer** study was located in this targeted search. This is a search limitation, not proof none exists. The strongest eligible extra below uses genetically hyperuricemic mouse donors. The second is useful but uses aging donors, not disease-selected donors.

### 1. Animal disease-donor whole FMT: hyperuricemia phenotype

**Song et al. (2022). Alteration of Gut Microbiome and Correlated Amino Acid Metabolism Contribute to Hyperuricemia and Th17-Driven Inflammation in Uox-KO Mice.** *Frontiers in Immunology*. DOI **10.3389/fimmu.2022.804306**. PMID **35197978**. [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8858814/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.804306).

- **Donor → recipient:** male **Uox-knockout (uricase-null) C57BL/6J mice**, which spontaneously develop hyperuricemia/urate-associated renal pathology, → male **wild-type C57BL/6J mice**, using whole fecal microbiota. Main FMT methods do **not report antibiotic or germ-free conditioning**.
- **Outcome:** WT FMT recipients had significantly higher **serum urate, urinary urate, and serum creatinine** than normal WT controls. Kidney index, urinary creatinine, total protein, and albumin did not differ significantly.
- **Controls / interpretation:** comparator was normal WT mice; a matched WT-donor FMT control is not clearly described. A separate cohousing arm also increased WT serum urate, but the paper independently contains an actual FMT arm. Count this as **transfer of hyperuricemia and a limited renal biochemical phenotype**, not complete gout or proven transfer of chronic urate nephropathy. MSU-induced arthritis findings in the original donor-model characterization should not be conflated with arthritis in FMT recipients.

### 2. Supplementary susceptibility evidence: aged-donor whole FMT worsens experimentally triggered gout

**Song N et al. (2025). Microbiota from young mice counteracts susceptibility to age-related gout through modulating butyric acid levels in aged mice.** *eLife*. DOI **10.7554/eLife.98714**. PMID **39907694**. [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11798573/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.98714).

- **Donor → recipient:** pooled whole fecal microbiota from **old/aged male C57BL/6 mice → young male C57BL/6 mice**, after recipient **antibiotic depletion and washout**. The study also includes protective reciprocal young-donor transfers.
- **Outcome:** old/aged-donor microbiota increased **monosodium-urate-challenged footpad swelling and inflammatory infiltration** in young recipients; the aged-donor arm increased inflammatory cytokines and serum urate. This is a harmful whole-transfer arm even though the title emphasizes the protective reverse direction.
- **Controls / interpretation:** age-matched PBS recipient controls and cross-age donor comparisons. **MSU challenge was required to test arthritis**; this demonstrates modified susceptibility/severity, not spontaneous gout caused by FMT. Donors were selected by age, not human gout status or necessarily animal gout disease, so keep separate from disease-donor transmission evidence. Publication is 2025 (earlier reviewed preprint in 2024).

### Excluded candidates / useful boundary

- Pan et al., *Abnormal metabolism of gut microbiota reveals the possible molecular mechanism of nephropathy induced by hyperuricemia* (2020; DOI 10.1016/j.apsb.2019.10.007; PMID 32082971; PMC7016297): full-text screen found **no fecal-transfer experiment**; association/metabolomics, not eligible causal transfer evidence.
- Therapeutic healthy-donor FMT into hyperuricemic animals and treatment-donor transfers were excluded; their protective results do not establish disease-donor transfer.
- Cell 2023 urate-metabolism gene-cluster work and healthy-human-donor purine metabolism variation are useful mechanisms but do not supply the requested gout-patient whole-stool harmful-transfer evidence.


## 11. Uveitis, asthma and pulmonary vascular disease


### E01 — Behçet disease, 2018

Ye Z et al. **A metagenomic study of the gut microbiome in Behcet's disease.** Microbiome, 2018. DOI **10.1186/s40168-018-0520-6**; PMID **30077182**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6091101/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30077182/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** H→A, pooled feces from active Behçet disease patients into B10RIII mice undergoing experimental autoimmune uveitis (EAU). Healthy controls were included in the human study; the transfer comparator and microbiota-depletion conditioning are not separately detailed in the abstract.
- **Endpoint:** worse EAU disease activity with higher IL-17 and IFN-γ.
- **Causal interpretation:** disease-associated community increases severity of experimentally initiated ocular autoimmunity. It does not demonstrate spontaneous Behçet disease, systemic vasculitis, oral/genital ulcers, or uveitis in otherwise unchallenged recipients. Donor pooling limits donor-level generalization.

### E02 — Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada disease, 2020

Ye Z et al. **Altered gut microbiome composition in patients with Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada disease.** Gut Microbes, 2020. DOI **10.1080/19490976.2019.1700754**; PMID **31928124**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7524263/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31928124/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** H→A, gut microbiota from VKH patients to mice undergoing EAU; healthy controls appear in the human comparison, while the transfer comparator is not separately detailed in the abstract. Exact recipient genotype and microbiota-conditioning details are not verified from the abstract.
- **Endpoint:** worsened clinical and pathological experimental uveitis.
- **Causal interpretation:** ocular autoimmunity exacerbation, not transfer of complete VKH syndrome. Particularly relevant to the hair-loss question: VKH is a multisystem disease, but this experiment's verified endpoint was uveitis; do not infer transfer of its potential alopecia/pigment manifestations.

### E03 — Behçet disease, 2021 mechanistic follow-up

Wang Q et al. **Changes in the Gut Microbiome Contribute to the Development of Behcet's Disease via Adjuvant Effects.** Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology, 2021. DOI **10.3389/fcell.2021.716760**; PMID **34568329**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8455896/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34568329/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** H→A, active ocular Behçet patient feces to mice; subsequent EAU or EAE induction. The transfer comparator and specific recipient background/depletion conditioning are not specified in the abstract.
- **Endpoint:** intestinal barrier disruption, circulating LPS and enhanced innate/Th1/Th17 responses; subsequent EAU and EAE were both worse.
- **Causal interpretation:** barrier/immune components plus a microbial adjuvant effect that amplifies experimentally triggered disease. It does not show spontaneous full Behçet syndrome. Worsening two autoimmune models favors a broader immune-amplification interpretation rather than disease-specific reproduction.

### L01 — Pulmonary arterial hypertension, 2025

Chen Y et al. **Multi-kingdom gut microbiota dysbiosis is associated with the development of pulmonary arterial hypertension.** EBioMedicine, 2025;115:105686. DOI **10.1016/j.ebiom.2025.105686**; PMID **40220715**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12013117/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40220715/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** both H→A (idiopathic-PAH patients→normal rats) and A→A (monocrotaline-PAH rats→normal rats). Reverse normal-rat→PAH-rat transfer also studied. Rat strain and microbiota-depletion conditioning are not specified in the abstract.
- **Endpoint:** the primary abstract explicitly reports induction of PAH phenotypes in recipient rats from both patient and model donors; reverse transfer ameliorated model PAH. Exact hemodynamic/histological measures were not independently verified from the abstract.
- **Causal interpretation:** positive disease-feature induction in recipients described as normal, a stronger directional design than rescue-only PAH studies. Whole-community results do not establish which kingdom or strain is independently sufficient; metagenomic associations with bacteria, archaea and fungi are not equivalent to individually proven causation. Two donor-source experiments are in one paper, not two independent publications.

### L02 — Human asthma microbiota, 2023

Wilson NG et al. **The gut microbiota of people with asthma influences lung inflammation in gnotobiotic mice.** iScience, 2023;26(2):105991. DOI **10.1016/j.isci.2023.105991**; PMID **36824270**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9941210/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36824270/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** human asthma versus healthy-control microbiota in gnotobiotic mice; the primary indexed search snippet identifies five donors per group and GF recipients. Exact recipient genotype is not established from the abstract. Effects are evaluated in allergic airway inflammation, not spontaneous clinical asthma.
- **Endpoint:** community-dependent modulation of lung inflammation and oxidative stress. The abstract emphasizes higher prevalence of enterotoxigenic Bacteroides fragilis in asthma donors and its dependence on surrounding community context.
- **Causal interpretation:** a verified human-donor mechanistic study, but **do not summarize as all asthma-donor stool uniformly recreating asthma**. The abstract does not supply an aggregate asthma-versus-control disease-score comparison sufficient to support that assertion. Keep the whole-community and isolated-organism findings separate.

### L03 — Human infant eczema communities and lung function: informative negative donor-disease specificity

Moya Uribe IA et al. **Fecal microbiota transplants (FMT) of three distinct human communities to germ-free mice exacerbated inflammation and decreased lung function in their offspring.** mBio, 2025. DOI **10.1128/mbio.03764-24**; PMID **40207915**. Primary: [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12077122/) ; [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40207915/) .

- **Donor/recipient:** eczemic infant, non-eczemic infant and adult human-derived communities in GF C57BL/6 mice, then offspring; mouse-community comparator. Both baseline and allergic conditions were examined.
- **Endpoint:** all three human-community groups had impaired lung mechanics versus mouse-community controls even without allergens; allergic challenges produced different inflammatory patterns in different human-community groups.
- **Causal interpretation:** strong warning against equating humanization effects with transfer of donor disease. The predicted eczemic-donor harm/non-eczemic-donor protection split was largely rejected. Impaired baseline lung function was not unique to the diseased donor. Ni et al., 2025. **Gut microbiota dysbiosis orchestrates vitiligo-related oxidative stress through the metabolite hippuric acid.** DOI **10.1186/s40168-025-02102-0**; PMID **40329424**. [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12054231/).

## 12. Alopecia, ME/CFS and other frequently overstated examples

**Alopecia has relevant causal biology, but the direction and disease subtype matter.** The 2017 Hayashi study demonstrates a nutrient–microbe mechanism for hair loss involving *Lactobacillus murinus* and biotin deprivation. That is not equivalent to transplanting an AA patient's fecal community and inducing autoimmune AA. The 2017/2019 human reports concern hair **regrowth** after FMT, and the 2025 mouse AGA report concerns hair-growth improvement. Those sources are documented in S11–S15. None justifies saying that AA or androgenetic alopecia has been shown to transmit through whole gut-community FMT in the studies verified here.

**ME/CFS:** the BioMapAI/multi-omics work is relevant to signatures and classification, but it is not a microbiota-transfer causality experiment. I did not verify a disease-donor whole-community experiment that reproduces ME/CFS as distinct from Long COVID, Gulf War illness or fibromyalgia. This is a search gap, not a declaration that microbes play no role. The relevant [BioMapAI primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03788-3) should not be counted as FMT symptom-transfer evidence.

**Seborrheic dermatitis:** no verified disease-donor **gut-community** transfer experiment was established in this search. Scalp microbiota, *Malassezia* associations and skin-colonization studies answer a different question.

**Celiac disease:** the verified human duodenal-community experiment transferred impaired gluten processing. It did not establish complete celiac enteropathy from fecal transfer. Likewise, autoimmune-hepatitis donor communities failed to induce hepatitis in a nonpredisposed GF host in the documented negative experiment.

**Therapeutic success does not automatically prove the reverse harmful transfer.** A community can restore a missing function without another community being sufficient to cause disease. Conversely, a harmful transfer need not imply that healthy FMT will reverse an established disease. Both directions must be tested.

## 13. Actual human transmission and what the animal work means for donors

### Infection: directly documented human-to-human transmission

**DeFilipp et al., 2019 — Drug-Resistant E. coli Bacteremia Transmitted by Fecal Microbiota Transplant.** Two recipients developed ESBL-producing *E. coli* bacteremia after material from the same donor; one died. Genomic investigation linked the organisms to donor material. This is direct transmission of an infectious organism, a different evidentiary category from animal transfer of a complex chronic phenotype. [Primary NEJM report](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1910437).

### Weight gain after human FMT: suggestive case, not causal demonstration

**Alang and Kelly, 2015 — Weight Gain After Fecal Microbiota Transplantation.** A recipient gained substantial weight after FMT from an overweight donor. Recovery from recurrent infection, concurrent treatment and other factors confound attribution; donor–recipient microbiome sequencing and a controlled comparator were absent. It belongs in the catalogue as an adverse temporal observation, not proof that the donor transmitted obesity. [Primary case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4438885/).

### Implications supported by this evidence

1. **Disease-associated microbiomes can carry transferable functions.** This is an experimentally grounded concern, particularly where multiple donors, independent cohorts, functional controls and recipient pathology support the result.
2. **A donor's diagnosis is an imperfect proxy for those functions.** Examples here include tumor-promoting biofilms in some healthy donors, hypertension results that reverse the expected donor-status direction, and a UC twin study in which the healthy cotwin community produced worse experimental colitis.
3. **Recipient context can be decisive.** Diet, resident community, age, host genotype, prior injury and immune state changed results, sometimes reversing them.
4. **A disease-resemblance percentage is not a validated probability of transferring that disease.** Most catalogue studies do not provide a calibrated human donor-risk model, and a high or low abundance of one species cannot substitute for the tested strain/function/community.
5. **Pooling donors does not erase a harmful component.** The studies here do not establish that mixing communities reliably dilutes or neutralizes transmissible risks. Nor do they establish that every disease-associated profile should automatically exclude a donor.

These implications preserve the experimental findings while keeping the inference tied to what was actually measured. Human screening for known transmissible infections and research assessment of complex disease-associated functions are related but distinct tasks.

## 14. Replication, attribution and search limitations

### Findings that materially change the interpretation

- **Obesity:** classic transfers increased adiposity, while Neyrinck 2025 found community changes without the expected metabolic phenotype. Duca 2014 was **retracted** and is excluded from positive evidence.
- **IBD:** susceptible hosts often develop or worsen colitis, but most active-UC donor communities did not cause spontaneous colitis in WT GF mice in the 2025 experiment; the one inflammatory donor carried previously unrecognized *C. difficile*.
- **Cancer:** donor-associated community characteristics can predict tumor promotion better than the donor's CRC diagnosis. Most experiments require cancer predisposition, carcinogens or implanted tumor cells.
- **Autoimmunity:** lupus-associated hypertension transferred without core autoimmunity in one study; autoimmune-hepatitis communities did not initiate hepatitis in normal-genotype GF recipients.
- **Neurobehavior:** several studies show dependence on sex, mouse strain, developmental exposure and the specific test. A patient-versus-healthy difference sometimes reflects benefit from healthy microbes rather than harm relative to no-FMT controls.
- **Bone and vascular traits:** negative/reversed transfers prevent universal claims based simply on donor disease labels.

### What makes a transfer experiment more informative

A useful comparison holds recipient conditions constant while varying independently sampled donor communities. Stronger designs include multiple donors per group, donor-level statistical replication, randomized/blinded outcome assessment, cage/litter controls, documented engraftment, a healthy-community comparator, a baseline/no-community comparator where interpretable, and mechanistic removal/rescue tests. A pool from many donors remains one mixed community preparation; many recipient mice do not turn it into many independent human-donor replications.

Whole stool carries bacteria, phages, fungi, metabolites, host products and potentially residual drugs. An FMT effect alone does not identify which component caused it. Repeated transfer or bacterial isolation/functional perturbation can strengthen microbial attribution, but each of those is a different experiment. Germ-free animals also have unusual immune and developmental baselines, so successful human-to-GF transfer is not a direct estimate of effects in an adult human with an established microbiome.

A methodological review found that 36 of 38 published human-microbiota-associated rodent studies reported phenotype transfer, raising concerns about publication and experimental-design bias. That observation does not invalidate a well-controlled positive experiment; it explains why independent replication and negative results belong beside it. [Walter et al., 2020, methodological analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.12.025).

### Search and verification

Some long article titles are shortened for display; DOI/PMID links identify the complete primary record.

Searches covered PubMed/Europe PMC records, primary publisher articles, accessible full texts and cited-reference follow-up, using disease terms with fecal/faecal microbiota transplantation, cecal transfer, colonization, germ-free/gnotobiotic recipients, human-to-mouse/rat transfer, cohousing, induction, exacerbation and susceptibility. Searches spanned neurological, psychiatric, metabolic, nutritional, gastrointestinal, liver, cancer, autoimmune, dermatologic, allergic, pulmonary, cardiovascular, renal, reproductive, endocrine, bone, muscle and ocular conditions. Recent papers were checked through the cutoff; known retractions/corrections were retained where located.

Primary abstracts were verified for included records; full texts were checked where accessible and where needed to resolve important recipient-conditioning or endpoint ambiguities. Entries explicitly say when the accessible record did not resolve a detail. An abstract-level claim is not silently upgraded to a fully audited animal protocol. Preprints are labeled; overlapping cohorts, corrections and separate arms are not independent replications.

This is not a PRISMA review: there was no preregistration, duplicate independent screening of every record, formal risk-of-bias scoring or guaranteed capture of all non-English/unindexed studies. The positive catalogue emphasizes adverse feature transfer and conceptually distinct replications; it does not attempt to list every healthy-FMT rescue paper, metabolite intervention or microbiome-association paper. Absence from this catalogue means “not verified in this search,” not “proved impossible.”
